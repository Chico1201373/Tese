Abstract — The scarcity of Arabic -language educational tools 
tailored for university students, particularly in specialized fields like 
law and humanities, limits academic engagement and learning 
effectiveness. Mufakkir addresses this gap by providing an Arabic 
educational RAG -based chatbot designed to assist students with 
retrieving, summarizing, and interacting with academic content. The 
system is trained on a curated Arabic dataset of legal books collected 
from Ain Shams University, with synthetic question –context pairs 
generated for evaluation using an LLM. Mufakkir achieved 88% 
accuracy, 88% recall, and 0.78 nDCG@10 on this benchmark. Its 
embedding model ranked first on the Arabic RAG Leaderboard with 
a score of 0.65, reflecting strong retrieval performance. In ans wer 
generation, it outperformed major LLMs such as DeepSeek, 
LLAMA, and Qwen, scoring 0.79 in Answer-Ground Similarity and 
0.73 in Question -Answer Similarity. By combining fine -tuned 
embeddings, hybrid retrieval, and domain -specific evaluation data, 
Mufakkir delivers accurate, context-aware educational support. 
 
Keywords—Educational Assistant, Retrieval -Augmented Generation 
(RAG), embedding model,  question answering, Natural Language 
Processing (NLP). 
I. INTRODUCTION 
There is a growing need for Arabic -language educational 
systems capable of providing contextually relevant academic 
assistance. Existing tools [1],[2] often lack support for Arabic or 
fail to reflect the structure and terminology of localized curricula, 
particularly in fields like law and the humanities, resulting in 
reduced engagement and learning effectiveness. This paper 
introduces Mufakkir, an Arabic -language assistant built using 
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and Natural Language 
Processing (NLP ) techniques, trained on curated academic 
content. Mufakkir is designed to be accurate, context -aware, and 
accessible for students in Arabic -speaking academic 
environments, offering features such as question answering, 
document summarization, file upload with content extraction and 
answering, and quiz generation. 
Mufakkir represents a significant advancement in Arabic NLP 
by delivering a tailored educational chatbot that addresses the 
critical shortage of context -aware, curriculum-specific tools for 
Arabic-speaking university students. The main contributions of 
this work include the introduction of a fine -tuned embedding 
model optimized for Arabic academic texts, along with a hybrid 
retrieval strategy combining semantic and lexical approaches. The 
system provides robust features such as question answering, 
document summarization, and quiz generation to enhance student 
engagement and learning effectiveness in fields such as law and 
humanities. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that 
presents an Arabic -enabled RAG -based model designed 
specifically for educational assistance.. 
Another key innovation of this work lies in the construction of 
a domain -specific dataset; MufakkirTriples which is built from 
Arabic law books t aught for university students. To enable high -
quality evaluation, we generated synthetic question–context pairs 
using a large language model, producing a benchmark tailored to 
the law domain to fill a critical gap in Arabic -language academic 
NLP resources. 
In terms of performance, Mufakkir ranked first on the Arabic 
RAG Leaderboard outperforming existing models such as 
DeepSeek [3], LLaMA [4], and Qwen [5]. In answer generation, it 
also achieved higher scores in Answer -Ground Similarity and 
Question-Answer S imilarity, demonstrating its superiority in 
producing accurate and relevant responses. These results confirm 
Mufakkir’s effectiveness in delivering contextually grounded, 
educationally aligned answers within low -resource domains like 
Arabic law. 
The paper is structured as follows: Section II reviews related 
work on Arabic language assistants, RAG systems, and 
educational NLP applications to position Mufakkir within the 
current research. Section III explains the methodology, covering 
query processi ng, document chunking, embedding model fine -
tuning, hybrid retrieval, and techniques to reduce hallucination. 
Section IV outlines the dataset, including its collection, 
preprocessing, and the creation of 25,000 question-answer-context 
triples. Section V hi ghlights the evaluation of Mufakkir's 
performance in comparison to existing models. Finally, Section VI 
concludes with a discussion of the system’s contributions, current 
limitations, and future research opportunities.

II. RELATED WORK 
A. Arabic Language Assistants 
The development of intelligent assistants for Arabic-speaking 
users has gained traction due to the need for contextually relevant 
tools. Existing systems often leverage NLP to process Arabic text, 
focusing on tasks like question answering and text understanding. 
However, many lack support for localized educational content, 
particularly in specialized fields such as law and humanities, 
leading to reduced effectiveness in academic settings.  
Sharma et al. [1] presented aiXplain SDK, a toolkit designed 
to standardize the development of AI assets, including Arabic NLP 
systems for tasks like question answering. The toolkit supported 
multilingual processing but focused primarily on general-purpose 
applications, lacking deep integration with curriculum -specific 
academic content, such as legal or humanities texts, which limits 
its utility for university students. Almazrouei et al. [2] introduced 
the Falcon series of open language models, which demons trated 
strong performance in Arabic text processing. However, these 
models were not optimized for educational contexts, and their 
general-purpose nature resulted in limited handling of domain -
specific terminology, reducing their effectiveness for specializ ed 
academic tasks.  
B. Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Arabic 
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) emerged as a 
powerful approach for enhancing language models by integrating 
external knowledge retrieval, improving factual accuracy and 
context awareness. In the context of Arabic, RAG systems 
combined semantic embeddings and generative models to handle 
linguistic complexities like morphology and dialect variations. 
These systems are versatile but often not tailored for educational 
applications. The evolution of RAG built on advancements in 
transformer-based Large Language Models (LLMs), such as those 
introduced by Vaswani et al. [6]. They proposed the transformer 
architecture, enabling efficient natural language understanding for 
multilingual tasks, including Arabic. However, their work focused 
on model architecture rather than retrieval integration, limiting its 
direct applicability to RAG systems. 
El-Beltagy [7] explores RAG for Arabic, focusing on its ability 
to handle linguistic nuances through semantic embeddings. The 
study demonstrated improved retrieval for general Arabic texts but 
lacked optimization for academic domains, resulting in limited 
relevance for educational applications. Alsubhi [8] conducts a 
systematic analysis of RAG pipeline components for Arabic, 
identifying optimal configurations for retrieval and generation. 
While comprehensive, the study did not address educational use 
cases, and its generic approach missed curriculum -specific 
requirements. 
C. Semantic Search in Arabic RAG 
   Semantic search in Arabic RAG systems leveraged dense 
vector representations to retrieve relevant documents by capturing 
semantic relationships between queries and texts. These systems 
used embeddings to encode queries and documents, enabling 
similarity-based retrieval that handled linguistic nuances like 
synonyms and morphological variations in Arabic. Techniques 
such as cosine similarity and hybrid retrieval, combining semantic 
and keyword -based approaches, enhance retrieval accuracy for 
complex academic content. The study in [9] evaluated semantic 
search in Arabic RAG, highlighting its role in improving context-
aware retrieval. It demonstrated high retrieval accuracy for general 
Arabic texts but notes challenges in handling domain -specific 
academic content due to limited dataset diversity. Alroumi [10] 
further explored optimized embedding training for Arabic 
semantic search, achieving improved retrieval performance. 
However, the approach is computationally intensive and not 
specifically tailored for educational applications. 
 
 
D. Fine-Tuning Embeddings for Arabic 
     Fine-tuning embeddings is a key strategy for improving text 
understanding in Arabic RAG systems. By optimizing sentence 
representations, these methods enhanced the accuracy of retrieval 
and generation tasks, particularly for educational tools processing 
academic content. Alroumi [10] investigated embedding fine -
tuning for Arabic RAG, proposing techniques to optimize 
semantic similarity in high -dimensional spaces. While effective, 
the method required significant computational resources and 
lacked focus on educational contexts, limiting its direct 
applicability to student -facing tools. Nacar et al. [11] explored 
nested embedding learning to enhance semantic similarity in 
Arabic NLP, achieving robust performance in text understanding. 
However, their approa ch is not tailored for curriculum -specific 
tasks, and its complexity may hinder scalability in resource -
constrained settings. 
E. Educational Applications of Arabic NLP 
   NLP-based educational tools for Arabic focus on tasks like 
language learning and content analysis. While some systems 
support features like poetry analysis or dialect identification, few 
address curriculum-specific needs in academic settings.  Sharma 
et al. [1] described aiXplain SDK’s applications in educational 
NLP, including Arabic language learning tools. However, its 
general-purpose design lacked deep integration with academic 
curricula, limiting its effectiveness for specialized fields like law. 
Nacar et al. [11] propose NLP methods for educational content 
analysis, demonstrating potential in text summarization. Their 
work, however, focused on broad applications and did not address 
the specific needs of university students in disciplines like 
humanities. 
III. METHODOLOGY 
Mufakkir’s system architecture integrates multiple 
components to deliver a robust Arabic educational chatbot tailored 
for university students. The system combines query preprocessing, 
document chunking, a fine -tuned embedding model for hybrid 
retrieval, a cross-encoder re -ranker, and an LLM for answer 
generation, with mechanisms to prevent hallucinations. User 
queries are preprocessed for accurate interpretation, followed by 
retrieval of relevant academic content from a vector database of 
chunked documents. The fine-tuned Mufakkir_Embedding model, 
optimized for Arabic academic texts, enables hybrid retrieval 
combining semantic and lexical approaches. Retrieved documents 
are reranked for relevance, and the Qwen2-72B LLM [5] generates 
context-aware responses, summaries, or quizzes, validated against 
retrieved context to ensure factual accuracy. This architecture, 
illustrated in Fig . 1, ensures efficient and accurate support for 
educational tasks. The following subsections detail each 
component of the system. 
A. Query Processing 
     The system preprocesses user queries to ensure accurate 
interpretation and effective retrieval of relevant academic content. 
This involves a multi -step pipeline designed to handle the 
linguistic complexities of Arabic text, including punctuation 
normalization, stop -word removal, tokenization, and query 
expansion. Punctuation normalization removes or standardizes 
punctuation marks (e.g., converting multiple consecutive 
punctuation marks to a single mark) to reduce noise and ensure 
consistent query formatting, which is critical for matching Arabic 
queries with varied typographical styles. Stop -word removal 
eliminates common Arabic words that carry minimal semantic 
value, using a curated stop-word list tailored for Arabic academic 
texts to enhance retrieval focus on meaningful terms. Tokenization 
splits the query into individual tokens, leveraging an Arabic -
specific tokenizer to handle morphological variations, such as verb 
conjugations and noun inflections, ensuring accurate segmentation 
of complex words. Query expansion is occasionally applied to 
include synonyms or related terms using a domain -specific

B. Document Chunking 
     The system processes a corpus of over 80 Arabic academic 
books, primarily legal and humanities texts, by segmenting them 
into smaller, coherent chunks to facilitate efficient and accurate 
retrieval. This is achieved through a recursive chunking method , 
which hierarchically splits documents to maintain semantic 
integrity and achieve an optimal chunk size. Initially, each book is 
divided into sections based on structural markers, such as chapters 
or headings, identified using metadata extracted via Azure  
Document Intelligence. Each section is then recursively 
subdivided into smaller units, typically paragraphs or sentence 
groups, ensuring that each chunk contains semantically cohesive 
content, such as a complete legal argument or a thematic 
discussion in humanities. The chunk size is optimized to balance 
granularity and context, targeting approximately 200 –300 words 
per chunk to align with the embedding model’s input constraints 
and ensure compatibility with the vector database. To preserve 
semantic integrity, the algorithm avoids splitting sentences or key 
phrases, using natural language processing techniques to detect 
sentence boundaries and semantic units in Arabic text, which is 
particularly challenging due to its morphological complexity. 
These chunks are then indexed in a Chroma DB vector database, 
where each chunk is encoded as a dense vector using the 
Mufakkir_Embedding model. Metadata, such as book title, 
chapter, and page number, is attached to each chunk to enable 
traceability and enhance retrieval precision. This approach ensures 
that the system can efficiently retrieve contextually relevant 
content for user queries, as described in Fig. 2.

E. Document Reranking 
   The system employs a cross -encoder re-ranker to enhance 
the relevance of retrieved documents by scoring query -document 
pairs and sorting them in descending order of relevance. Unlike bi-
encoder models [13], which independently encode queries and 
documents into separate embeddings and compute similarity (e.g., 
via cosine similarity), the cross-encoder takes both the query and 
document as a single input, processing them jointly through a 
transformer-based architecture to capture deep contextual 
interactions. This joint encoding allows the cross-encoder to model 
complex semantic relationships, such as syntactic dependencies 
and contextual nuances in Arabic academic texts, resulting in more 
accurate relevance scores. For each query -document pair, the 
cross-encoder outputs a scalar relevance score, t ypically between 
0 and 1, which is used to rank the top retrieved documents. The 
highest-scoring documents are passed to the generation phase, 
ensuring that only the most contextually relevant content informs 
the Qwen2-72B LLM’s responses. The cross -encoder’s superior 
accuracy over bi-encoder models stems from its ability to leverage 
full query -document interactions, though it is computationally 
more intensive. In Mufakkir, this trade-off is justified by the need 
for precise retrieval in academic settings, where relevance is 
critical for tasks like answering complex legal or humanities 
questions. This approach significantly improves retrieval 
performance, as described in [8]. 
 
F. Answer Generation 
    The Qwen2 -72B LLM, a transformer -based large language 
model with 72 billion parameters, generates answers, summaries, 
or quizzes based on the user query and the top-ranked documents 
retrieved from the dataset. The model’s architecture leverages 
advanced at tention mechanisms to process input sequences, 
enabling it to understand and generate coherent text in multiple 
languages, including Arabic. In Mufakkir, the Qwen2 -72B takes 
as input the preprocessed user query concatenated with the context 
from reranked document chunks, typically formatted as a prompt 
that instructs the model to produce a specific output type (e.g., a 
direct answer, a concise summary, or a set of quiz questions with 
answers). For instance, for a query requesting a summary of a legal 
concept, the model generates a condensed explanation by 
synthesizing relevant information from the retrieved chunks. Its 
multilingual capabilities are particularly suited for complex Arabic 
academic tasks due to its training on diverse datasets, including 
Arabic texts. This enables handling linguistic nuances such as 
morphological variations and domain -specific terminology in 
legal and humanities contexts.  
The model’s large parameter size enhances its ability to 
generate contextually accurate and fluent responses, critical for 
educational applications where precision and clarity are 
paramount. Additionally, the Qwen2 -72B’s fine -tuning for 
instruction-following ensures that it adheres to the prompt’s 
requirements, producing outputs tailored to the user’s intent, such 
as generating multiple -choice quizzes for student practice. This 
approach ensures robust performance for Mufakkir’s educational 
features, as described in [8]. 
 
G. Hallucination Prevention 
     A specialized prompt template instructs the LLM to 
validate responses against retrieved context, regenerating or 
revising answers if inconsistencies are detected. This ensures 
factual integrity is critical for academic applications [5]. 
 
H. Summary 
     Mufakkir’s methodology integrates query preprocessing, 
recursive document chunking, and a finetuned embedding model 
for hybrid retrieval. A Cross encoder re-ranker refines results, and 
the Qwen2 -72B LLM generates context -aware outputs. 
Hallucination control ensures factual accuracy, making the system 
robust for Arabic educational tasks. 
 
    IV. DATASET 
 
A. Collection Challenges 
     Creating a domain-specific Arabic academic dataset posed 
challenges due to the limited availability of structured educational 
resources. Manual collection of materials from faculties was time-
consuming, particularly for non -digitized texts. The absence of 
standardized Arabic datasets for question answering and semantic 
search necessitated synthetic data generation to simulate academic 
scenarios [15]. 
 
B. Dataset Description 
Our MufakkirTriples  dataset comprises 80 academic books 
and lecture notes from various university departments, primarily 
focusing on legal and humanities texts, yielding approximately 
25,000 question–answer–context triples. Each triple consists of a 
question, a corresponding  answer, and a context derived from a 
document chunk, designed to reflect real -world challenges faced 
by Arabic -speaking university students. For example, a triple 
might include a question about the conditions for a valid contract 
in Egyptian law, an answe r summarizing the legal requirements, 
and a context extracted from a relevant section of a legal textbook. 
Fig. 4 presents an example data sample from the 
MufakkirTriples dataset, illustrating (a) a context excerpt from an 
academic textbook, ( b) the generated question based on the 
context, and (c) the corresponding academic answer. These triples 
were generated using Qwen 70B Instruct with a carefully designed 
prompt template to ensure academic relevance, answer accuracy, 
and semantic coherence with the source document.

C. Dataset Preprocessing 
     The MufakkirTriples dataset is prepared through a multi -
step preprocessing pipeline to ensure quality and suitability for 
retrieval tasks. Documents are segmented into coherent chunks 
using recursive chunking to maintain semantic integrity and enable 
efficient retrieval, resulting in approximately 50,000 chunks across 
the 80 academic books and lecture notes. Each chunk is targeted 
to contain around 600 tokens, a size chosen to balance semantic 
coherence with the input constraints of the Mufakkir_Embedding 
model and the Chroma DB vector database. An overlap of 200 
tokens is applied between consecutive chunks to ensure contextual 
continuity, preventing the loss of semantic connections across 
chunk boundaries. This overlap means that the last 200 tokens of  
one chunk are included as the first 200 tokens of the next chunk, 
allowing for seamless retrieval of related content when queries 
span chunk boundaries.  
Azure Document Intelligence [16] is employed to extract 
structured content from PDFs and scanned materials, preserving 
academic layouts such as headings, sections, and tables, which are 
critical for maintaining the integrity of academic texts. Each chunk 
is annotated with metadata, including book titles, chapter 
identifiers, and page numbers, to enhance response traceability and 
trustworthiness, ensuring that retrieved content can be linked back 
to its source for verification [9].

                       VI. CONCLUSION & FUTURE WORK 
 
The introduction of Mufakkir  represents a groundbreaking 
advancement in the field of Arabic NLP, specifically tailored to 
the educational domain. By integrating State -Of-The-Art RAG 
techniques, the chatbot has successfully combined fine -tuned 
embedding models, hybrid retrieval strategies, and advanced large 
language models to deliver superior performance in both retrieval 
and generation tasks. The system's notable achievements, 
including first place on the Arabic RAG Leaderboard and high 
scores in metrics such as Accuracy@5, Recall@5, and nDCG@10, 
underscore its technical robustness and practical relevance. 
In answer generation, Mufakkir has shown exceptional 
capabilities in producing context -aware responses that align 
closely with user queries, as evidenced by its high scores in AGS 
and QAS. These strengths position Mufakkir as a pivotal tool for 
enhancing Arabic academic learning by bridging the gap in NLP 
tools for Arabic -speaking users. However, challenges remain, 
particularly in improving Faithfulness, where ensuring that 
generated responses adhere strictly to retrieved and factual 
information is a critical next step. 
Looking ahead, future research will explore ways to address 
these limitations, including refining the system's faithfulness 
metrics and extending its applicability across a wider range of 
academic disciplines. Another important avenue of development 
is the incorporation of multilingual capabilities, enabling Mufakkir 
to serve diverse linguistic audiences and foster cross -cultural 
academic engagement. Additionally, expanding its dataset to 
encompass more specialized and interdisciplinary academic 
content wil l further strengthen its utility as an educational 
resource. 
Ultimately, Mufakkir's contributions not only demonstrate the 
potential of RAG systems in advancing Arabic NLP but also lay 
the foundation for developing intelligent, accessible, and impactful 
educational tools for Arabic -speaking learners worldwide. Its 
success highlights the transformative power of integrating cutting-
edge AI technologies into education, paving the way for future 
innovations that address the unique needs of underrepresented 
languages in the digital age.
