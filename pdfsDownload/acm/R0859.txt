Abstract
With the rapid development of large language model (LLM) tech-
nologies, general-purpose models face prominent problems such
as knowledge hallucinations, information lag, and insufficient hier-
archical adaptation in knowledge Q&A for professional fields like
power electronics. Aiming at the teaching scenario of the under-
graduate course Power Electronics Technology, this paper designs
andimplements a Q&Aagent based on retrieval-augmented genera-
tion (RAG) and prompt engineering technologies via the DeepSeek
API. By constructing an undergraduate professional knowledge
base, adopting a hybrid retrieval strategy, and applying prompt en-
gineering, the Q&A agent effectively overcomes the shortcomings
of general-purpose models in professional term accuracy, knowl-
edge timeliness, and answer hierarchical adaptation. Experimental
results show that the system achieves high accuracy and stability in
undergraduate power electronics knowledge Q&A tasks, providing
a feasible solution for the intellectualization of professional course
teaching.

2 RELATED WORK
2.1 Research Status and Applications of RAG
Technology
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) significantly improves the
accuracy and reliability of model answers in professional fields
by combining external knowledge retrieval with LLM generation.
In recent years, RAG has evolved from the basic “retrieval + gen-
eration” paradigm into various enhanced variants. For general
RAG evaluation, CRUD-RAG builds a large-scale comprehensive
benchmark covering four RAG application scenarios: Create, Read,
Update, and Delete. It systematically assesses the impacts of re-
trievers, context length, knowledge base construction, and LLMs
on RAG performance, offering guidance for RAG optimization in
different scenarios. [1]
In other vertical domains, RAG has been successfully applied
to typhoon track prediction (combining LLM and RAG to achieve
12-hour mean absolute error of 10.78 km) [4] and vision-language
navigation in continuous environments (the RAGNav method im-
proves navigation success rate by 2%–2.32%). [2] These studies
confirm that RAG can effectively enhance LLM performance in
specific tasks, but its knowledge base content and retrieval strategy
must be deeply adapted to domain characteristics.

3 PROMPT ENGINEERING AND KNOWLEDGE
BASE CONSTRUCTION
3.1 Construction of Undergraduate Power
Electronics Knowledge Base
The knowledge base of this system is derived from classroom teach-
ing recordings of the undergraduate course Power Electronics Tech-
nology. The instructor is a senior professor with long-term expe-
rience in power electronics teaching and research. The content
covers core knowledge points such as power electronic devices and
integrates extensive teaching insights.
First, 60classhours(about3000minutes)ofteachingvideoswere
collected via classroom recording equipment. Then, the iFlytek
Hear voice-to-text tool automatically converted the teacher’s expla-
nations into text to generate raw corpus. To improve knowledge
accuracy, manual proofreading was performed on the transcription
results. Professional terms (e.g., “IGBT”, “PWM”) were corrected
sentence by sentence, finally yielding about 340,000 high-quality
words. The text was split into 713 independent knowledge units by
knowledge points, each containing a question and answer, serving
as the underlying knowledge base for retrieval-augmented gen-
eration. This knowledge base closely aligns with actual course
teaching, ensuring high consistency between the Q&A assistant
and classrooms in professional terms and key teaching points.
The knowledge base adopts a Question & Answer (Q&A) struc-
ture. Each knowledge entry consists of a typical question (e.g.,
“What is the main function of a drive circuit?”).
3.2 Prompt Engineering Design
Prompt engineering is key to constraining LLM behavior and adapt-
ing it to specific task scenarios. For the undergraduate Power
Electronics Technology Q&A scenario, this system designed a hi-
erarchical, iterable system prompt. Through multiple rounds of
optimization, it effectively addressed deficiencies of general mod-
els in term standardization, ambiguity handling, and knowledge
boundary control. [5]

3.3 Iterative Optimization of Prompts
To solve the above problems, this paper enhanced the prompt
through multiple iterations, adding the following key rules:
Wrong term and ambiguity handlingAdd a questioning mecha-
nism: if the question contains wrong or uncommon professional
terms (e.g., “cosine sampling method” (non-standard term)), the
model should ask the user to confirm term correctness; if the ques-
tion is unclear or ambiguous (e.g., without specifying device type),
explain or ask questions through multi-turn dialogue.
Randomness control and format specificationSet API parameter
temperature=0.1 to reduce generation randomness. Keep answers
professional, rigorous, and easy to understand.
After the above optimization, the system’s performance on typi-
cal questions improved significantly (Table 2).
Practice proves that prompt engineering is critical in RAG ap-
plications. Even with a high-quality knowledge base and retrieval
module, poorly designed system prompts lead to non-standard
terms, ambiguities, blurred knowledge boundaries, and other is-
sues. Through multi-round iterative optimization, prompts not
only constrain the answer scope but also actively guide interaction
behavior, significantly improving the accuracy, safety, and teaching
adaptability of the Q&A agent.

4 SYSTEM PROCESS AND ARCHITECTURE
DESIGN
4.1 Overall Architecture
The system adopts a modular design with the overall architecture
as follows:
Data layer: Responsible for construction and management of the
undergraduate power electronics knowledge base.
Service layer: Includes retrieval module, generation module, and
dialogue management module.
Application layer: Provides user interaction interface and Q&A
service API.
4.2 Technology Selection
DeepSeek API (DeepSeek-V3), called via HTTP interface.
Vectordatabase: File-basedlightweightstorage. Knowledgebase
fragments are semantically vectorized and persistently stored in a
local file (vector_db.pkl) using pickle serialization. During retrieval,
all vectors are loaded into memory, linear search is performed via
cosine similarity, and Top-K relevant fragments are returned.
Development framework: Flask as the Web backend; native
HTML/CSS/JavaScript for the frontend interaction interface. The
backendprovidesQ&AservicesviaRESTfulAPIandmanagesmulti-
turn dialogue states using Flask Session. The overall architecture is
lightweight and easy to deploy, suitable for teaching scenarios.
4.3 Retrieval Module Design
The retrieval module uses a hybrid retrieval strategy: semantic re-
trieval + BM25. Semantic retrieval maps questions to vectors using
the aforementioned vectorization model and calculates cosine simi-
larity with knowledge base fragments. BM25 performs keyword
matching based on term frequency-inverse document frequency.

5 EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN AND EV ALUATION
5.1 Experimental Objectives and Overall Design
To fully verify the effectiveness of the Q&A agent, experiments
focus on three core dimensions:
Accuracy: Compare answer accuracy and hallucination rate
with/without RAG enhancement and prompt engineering optimiza-
tion.
Adaptability: Verify the course specificity of the knowledge base
(vs. general knowledge base) and the effect of prompt engineering
on term standardization and hierarchy control.
Interactivity: Evaluate contextual understanding and anaphora
resolution in multi-turn dialogue scenarios.
The experiment uses a control variable method, sets multiple
comparison groups, uses the same test question set, and evaluates
via manual scoring and automatic metrics.
5.2 Experimental Dataset
An undergraduate power electronics Q&A test set is constructed,
including:
Concept explanation (e.g., “What is a frequency converter?”);
Principle analysis (e.g., “Working principle of Buck circuit”);
Standard specification.

6 CONCLUSION
ThispaperpresentsanRAG-basedQ&Aagentforpowerelectronics
education, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy,
timeliness, and level appropriateness. Current limitations include
narrow knowledge coverage and lack of multimodal support. Fu-
ture work will expand the knowledge base, integrate circuit dia-
gram recognition, and incorporate user feedback for continuous
optimization.
Aiming at the problems of general large language models in
undergraduate power electronics knowledge Q&A, this paper de-
signs and implements an intelligent Q&A assistant system based on
RAG technology. Main contributions: constructing a power elec-
tronics professional knowledge base for undergraduate teaching;
designing a hybrid retrieval and generation architecture suitable
for course Q&A scenarios; realizing precise control of knowledge
scope through prompt engineering.
This study still has the following limitations: knowledge base
coverage needs expansion; limited ability to process multimodal
content (circuit diagrams, waveform diagrams); answer quality for
complex reasoning problems still needs improvement. Subsequent
research will include: expanding the knowledge base to cover more
power electronics subfields; exploring multimodal RAG solutions
to support circuit diagram recognition and analysis; introducing
a user feedback mechanism to realize continuous learning and
optimization.
