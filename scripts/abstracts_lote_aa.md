**Abstract:**

Large Language Models (LLMs) are advanced artificial intelligence systems which are designed to generate human-like language based on their training data. Retrieval- Augmented Generation (RAG) is a framework that enhances the response of LLM by incorporating external information sources, leading to more accurate, context-relevant responses. However, LLMs commonly struggle with contextual relevance, especially in particular fields such as legal documentation, where specific local laws are crucial. ## STraVEns: Sentence Transformer Voting Ensemble for Intent Classification-Based Chatbot Model

**TL;DR:** This study suggests that ensemble-based approaches can enhance the performance of intent classification chatbots model, providing a scalable solution for various applications.

**Abstract:**

Natural Language Processing has experienced significant advancements in recent years, leading to the widespread adoption of Large Language Model-based chatbots. These chatbots are popular due to their ability to engage in context-aware conversations. However, deploying LLM-based chatbots can be resource-intensive, making them less suitable for smaller applications or focused tasks. To address this issue, we propose a robust and flexible approach to intent classification for chatbots using STraVEns (Sentence Transformer Voting Ensemble), which includes both hard voting and soft voting ensembles of sentence transformers. Our proposed method aims to improve accuracy and versatility in intent-based chatbots model. We use five sentence transformer models for this ensemble framework: RoBERTa, DistilRoBERTa, MPNet, MiniLM L6, and MiniLM L12, and evaluated our approach by training and testing using four distinct datasets: ATIS, IDE, Small Talk, and CLINC150 which cover a range of scenarios from general conversation to specific tasks and out-of-scope intent classification. The results demonstrate that the STraVEns approach is a promising solution for intent classification-based chatbot model. Results show that our ensemble models outperformed previous benchmarks, achieving the highest accuracy and F1-scores across all datasets. The soft voting method provided flexibility and robustness, while hard voting ensured stability in specific contexts. Overall, our study suggests that ensemble-based approaches can enhance the performance of intent classification chatbots model, providing a scalable solution for various applications.

**Abstract:**

Standard recommendation systems, which rely on static databases and historical user patterns, are fundamentally ill-suited for the dynamic and informal nature of Community-Based Tourism (CBT). In these environments, “items” such as local vendor schedules and seasonal activities are highly volatile and often undocumented, causing generic Large Language Models (LLMs) to frequently “hallucinate” or fabricate services that do not exist. This study presents a novel Tourism-Specific Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) Agent designed to bridge this gap. Unlike traditional recommenders that optimize for engagement, researchers' architecture optimizes for factual trustworthiness, integrating an agentic planner that autonomously retrieves real-time community data before generating advice. Researchers validated this approach through a comparative benchmark against a standard LLM baseline using 50 simulated traveler personas. The results quantify the critical risk of generic models: while the Base LLM achieved a dangerous Faithfulness score of only 0.25 (frequently inventing venues), researchers' RAG Agent achieved 0.56 Faithfulness and 0.60 Relevancy, with a complete elimination of unsafe recommendations. This work demonstrates that reliable tourism recommendation requires a specialized architecture capable of verifying ephemeral local knowledge, distinguishing it from general-purpose generative AI.

**Abstract:**

Border inspection legal question answering (LQA) is designed for specific legal scenarios, aiming to address legal questions related to border inspections and provide accurate and practical legal guidance to the public and border inspection departments. Current research requires a retrieval process regardless of whether the necessary knowledge already exists, and does not evaluate the legal information in the generated responses, leading to errors and low-quality content. we propose the Dynamic Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework for Border Inspection LQA (DRAG-BILQA), which generates responses first and then dynamically controls retrieval based on confidence scores. In this framework, we innovatively introduce a legal factor recognition module to ensure the legal accuracy of generated answers and improve the quality of the generation. We also present the BorderLegal-QA dataset, filling the gap in border inspection legal datasets. We conducted extensive experiments on both the BorderLegal-QA and the general LQA dataset JEC-QA. **Abstract:**

Multimodal retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) improves evidence-grounded question answering, but many existing systems still rely mainly on textual retrieval and weakly handle visual, OCR, table, and metadata evidence. This paper proposes a lightweight MLLM-driven multimodal RAG framework following an “Input-Retrieve-Fuse-Reason-Output” pipeline. Given a query, visual, textual, and OCR-based retrievers collect modality-specific candidates. A lightweight evidence fusion module then normalizes retrieval scores, considers query relevance and cross-modal alignment, penalizes redundancy, and selects a compact multimodal context for MLLM reasoning. Experiments on WebQA and MultiModalQA show consistent improvements over direct MLLM answering, text-only RAG, multimodal retrieval without fusion, score-normalized multimodal RAG, and cross-modal reranking multimodal RAG. The framework achieves 60.7/62.5/68.9/83.4 on WebQA and 69.2/71.4/79.3/89.6 on MultiModalQA in terms of EM/Acc./F1/ER, showing improved answer quality and evidence reliability.

## A Multi-Agent Graph RAG Framework with ReAct and A2A Protocol

**Autores:** Nitij Taneja, Trapti Sharma  
**Publicação:** 2026 IEEE 1st International Conference on Instrumentation (INSTCon) · 2026  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/INSTCon69741.2026.11691713  
**Fonte:** ieee — https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11691713/  
**Keywords:** Turning, Printing, Large language models, Modeling, Aluminum, Context, Knowledge graphs, Cognition, Cognitive systems, Retrieval augmented generation, Prosecution, Hallucinations, Failure Modes, Context Window, Punishment, Low Precision, Text Classification, Graph Traversal, Indian Penal Code, Graph RAG, Multi-Agent Systems, ReAct, A2A Protocol, TF-IDF, Knowledge Graph, Legal Question Answering, BNS Context Drift, Memory Degradation, LLM Evaluation  

**Abstract:**

Legal question answering over the Indian Penal Code 1860 (IPC) demands precision, structured reasoning, and source verifiability. This work identifies two failure modes in LLM-based IPC retrieval. First, multi-turn context degradation: LLM accuracy declines substantially across extended dialogue as earlier legal context is progressively displaced within the context window. Second, corpus context drift: all four evaluated LLMs spontaneously cite Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023 (BNS) section numbers when answering IPC-targeted queries despite explicit IPC-only instructions—an out-of-scope hallucination caused by LLMs mapping to the current statutory code.We propose a Multi-Agent Graph RAG Framework comprising: (i) a typed Knowledge Graph of 598 nodes and 15,430 relational edges from 575 IPC sections; (ii) TF-IDF sparse retrieval for jargon-precise section identification; and (iii) a ReAct-driven Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocol with three parallel specialised agents (Researcher, Prosecutor, Defense).Evaluation on 30 LLM baseline queries finds Full Section Accuracy of 63.3–98.3%, with every LLM producing BNS citations for 10 queries. An ablation study over 100 queries (10 turns × 10 queries per turn) shows the Knowledge Graph contributes +8.0 pp Hit@5 (p=0.013) and EXCEPTION_OF edges +7.0 pp (p=0.023). The Multi-Agent A2A protocol contributes 0 pp to retrieval coverage but improves qualitative answer structure. The framework achieves 94% overall multi-turn accuracy, 100% T10 retention, and zero BNS corpus drift across 100 queries.

## A Retrieval-Augmented Generation Framework for Question Answering over Product Manuals

**Abstract:**

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language processing tasks. However, in intelligent question-answering (QA) applications within vertical domains, they are highly prone to hallucinations due to a lack of domain-specific knowledge. Retrieval-Augmented Generation effectively mitigates this issue by integrating external knowledge bases. In this paper, we propose a RAG framework tailored for product manual QA. First, we utilize LLMs to convert illustrations within the manuals into natural language descriptions, and perform hierarchical chunking on the manual content based on Markdown heading structures to construct a knowledge base dedicated to product documents. Subsequently, a hierarchical document routing method is employed to identify the specific product manual targeted by the user's query, thereby narrowing the retrieval scope. Building upon this, a hybrid retrieval approach—combining keyword retrieval and semantic vector retrieval—is applied to extract the top-K relevant chunks. Finally, LLM generates a comprehensive response containing both textual explanations and relevant illustrations based on the retrieved results. Experimental results demonstrate that, compared to baseline methods, our proposed approach improves the final score from 0.7286 to 0.7943, validating the effectiveness of this framework in product manual QA scenarios.

## A Systematic Literature Review of Prompt Injection Attacks in LLM-Integrated Systems

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3700492  
**Fonte:** ieee — https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11551573/  
**Citações:** 1  
**Keywords:** Conferences, Large language models, Licenses, Modeling, Nuclear facility regulation, Security, Codes, Tools, Reviews, Weighted sum model, Systematic Review, Elsevier, Digital Library, Properties Of Model, Research Orientation, IEEE Xplore, Defence Research, Attack Surface, ACM Digital Library, Agentic, Search String, Application Programming Interface, Inference Time, Types Of Attacks, Cyber-physical Systems, Ultimate Objective, Secondary Goal, Attack Target, Threat Model, Single Paper, Multiple Boundaries, Function Calls, Persistent Memory, Context Window, Chatbot, Attack Success Rate, Sequence Of Tokens, Inconsistent Terminology, Alter Ego, Prompt injection, large language models, LLM security, trust boundaries, adversarial attacks, MITRE ATLAS, jailbreaking, attack-defence analysis, retrieval-augmented generation, systematic literature review  

**TL;DR:** The corpus is dominated by research on the prompt interface and by two attacker objectives, Instruction Hijack and Policy Bypass, leaving underexplored boundaries lacking both attack characterization and defence proposals.

**Abstract:**

Prompt injection exploits a fundamental property of large language models: the inability to distinguish trusted instructions from untrusted content when both are processed as natural language tokens. As these models are integrated into tool-augmented and memory-enabled systems, the attack surface expands beyond the prompt interface, yet no existing review maps this landscape against standardised adversarial frameworks or analyses the balance between offensive and defensive research. This review follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. We searched IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, and ScienceDirect for peer-reviewed studies published between 2015 and October 2025, yielding 89 included publications of which 81 form the analytical corpus. Aligned to the MITRE Adversarial Threat Landscape for AI Systems framework (version 5.4.0), each paper was coded along three axes: trust boundary, attacker objective, and research orientation with attacker objectives further classified into seventeen operational patterns aligned to MITRE ATLAS techniques. The corpus is dominated by research on the prompt interface and by two attacker objectives, Instruction Hijack and Policy Bypass, leaving underexplored boundaries lacking both attack characterization and defence proposals. This architectural narrowness does not reflect the multi-component architecture of production LLM systems. The three-axis framework and coded corpus establish a baseline for tracking how coverage evolves as deployment architectures expand. The concentration reflects a research incentive structure that favours reproducible prompt-level experiments over architecturally complex assessments at tool and memory boundaries. The field exhibits a maturity gradient in which defence development is concentrated at the prompt interface while other boundaries remain in early or absent characterization. The corpus treats prompt injection as a model-level property rather than a system-level vulnerability.

## A Systematic Review of Retrieval-Augmented Generation Techniques for Mitigating Hallucinations in Chatbot Systems

**Autores:** Shahirah Mohamed Hatim, Afif Amsyar Khairul Nizam, Nurhawa Shamsudin, Nur Azmina Mohamad Zamani, Zaaba Ahmad  
**Publicação:** 2026 7th International Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Data Sciences (AiDAS) · 2026  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/AiDAS71914.2026.11712729  
**Fonte:** ieee — https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11712729/  
**Keywords:** Retrieval augmented generation, Modeling, Printing, Reviews, Conferences, Grounding, Large language models, Architecture, Computer architecture, Context, Hallucinations, Benchmark, Removal Of Duplicates, Language Model, Evaluation Practices, Inference Time, External Knowledge, IEEE Xplore, Retrieval Strategy, Preprint, Evaluation Framework, Mechanical Integrity, Modular Design, Usage Intention, Heterogeneous Datasets, Correctly Answered, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, hallucination mitigation, large language models, chatbot systems, systematic literature review, information retrieval  

**Abstract:**

Large language model (LLM) chatbots are increasingly deployed in education, health care, and decision-support settings, yet their reliability is limited by hallucination: fluent responses that are factually incorrect, weakly grounded, or inconsistent with supplied evidence. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) addresses this limitation by conditioning generation on external knowledge retrieved at inference time. This paper presents a systematic review of RAG techniques for hallucination mitigation in chatbot systems. Following the PRISMA 2020 process, studies published from 2020 to 2026 were searched in Scopus, IEEE Xplore, and Google Scholar. After duplicate removal, screening, and full-text eligibility assessment, 23 studies were synthesized qualitatively. The review analyzes RAG architectures, retrieval strategies, evidence-integration mechanisms, and evaluation practices. Findings show that advanced and modular RAG architectures generally improve factuality and faithfulness over purely parametric LLMs, especially when retrieval is paired with re-ranking, evidence filtering, verification, or citation-aware generation. However, mitigation remains constrained by retrieval noise, latency, limited benchmark standardization, and weak integration between hallucination detection and correction. The paper concludes with research directions for trustworthy, efficient, and explainable RAG-based chatbot systems.

## AI Prompt Injection Attack Tester

**Autores:** Likitha R, Dhanyashree D C, Lavanya H R, Preeti

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/ESCI68015.2026.11493291

**Keywords:** Feedback, Filtering, Circuits, Filters, Graphical user interfaces, Product development, Internet, Bot (Internet), Protocols, Communication systems, Risk Score, User Input, Input Files, PDF Files, Malicious Intent, Multimodal Interaction, Text Extraction, Multimodal Detection, Groups Of Keywords, Input Categories, Prompt Injection, LLM Security, OCR, AI Safety, Streamlit, Multi-Modal Detection  

**Abstract:**

Large Language Models are widely integrated into chatbots, enterprise applications, and automation systems. However, these systems are vulnerable to prompt injection attacks - malicious instructions embedded in text, files, images, or translated content, attempting to override system rules. This paper presents the design and implementation of the AI Prompt Injection Attack Tester: a multi-modal system for detecting and mitigating Prompt Injection attempts against Large Language Models. While the ubiquitous deployment of LLMs has introduced a critical security vulnerability, where untrusted user input hijacks the model’s policy and system prompt, we introduce the following concept: a Heuristic-Based Prompt Injection Detection Engine integrated into a pre-processing guardrail layer. The PIDE applies a specialized ruleset, which includes instruction-overriding pattern matching and sensitive keyword identification, to incoming data for a risk score. Importantly, the system normalizes and scans inputs from four different modalities: direct text, embedded code comments/literals, image text via OCR, and text that has been processed through a mock translation service. On detection, a Mitigation Pipeline applies sequential steps of sanitization, instruction stripping, and policy-based blocking prior to any input reaching the LLM. As confirmed within a safe, mock environment for LLMs, experimental results validate that this approach provides both successful detection and neutralization of malicious instructions across all types of input tested in this work. This paper confirms that a robust, multi-modal pre-processing policy layer is required as an integral component of a broad defense-in-depth strategy against sophisticated Prompt Injection attacks.

**Abstract:**
