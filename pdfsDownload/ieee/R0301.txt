II.
 
L
ITERATURE 
R
EVIEW
 
Recent advances in 
LLM
 
systems have led to growing 
interest in collaborative and data
-
augmented approaches for 
complex reasoning tasks such as medical question answering. 
Due to the imperfection of current LLMs and the complexity of 
interdisciplinary factoring and reasoning in the medicine realm, 
multiple approaches have been proposed to improve the 
accuracy in medical question answering, such as
 
multi
-
agent 
collaboration, retrieval
-
augmented generation (RAG), and 
data
-
centric scaling effects.
 
A.
 
Multi
-
Agent Collaboration Frameworks
 
Multi
-
agent LLM frameworks have been developed to 
simulate distributed reasoning, where multiple specialized 
agents coordinate toward a shared objective. CAMEL 
[8]
 
and 
MetaGPT 
[9]
 
demonstrated structured role
-
based collaboration 
in software engineering and reasoning tasks, while ChatDev 
[10]
 
applied similar principles for cooperative code generation. 
These frameworks showed that explicit task division and 
communication protocols can improve consistency and task 
decomposition. However, most 
of current studies 
rely on 
homogeneous models and lack domain specialization or 
dynamic weighting mechanisms, leading to limited scalability 
in expert
-
driven fields such as medicine.
 
B.
 
Retrieval
-
Augmented Generation (RAG) and External 
Knowledge Integration
 
Retrieval
-
augmented methods, such as REALM 
[11]
 
and 
RETRO 
[12]
, combine retrieval and generation to enhance 
factual grounding. 
REPLUG
 
[13]
 
d
ecouples the retrieval and 
LLM training, allowing the LLM to learn how to better utilize 
retrieved documents without modifying the underlying 
retriever.
 
In medical question answering, RAG architectures 
have improved answer accuracy by leveraging biomedical 
knowledge bases and PubMed retrieval pipelines. However, 
RAG systems often depend heavily on retrieval quality and can 
suffer from hallucination when re
trieved evidence is 
incomplete or inconsistent.
 
C.
 
Impact of Data Scale and Curation on Model Performance
 
Recent work highlights that dataset quality often outweighs 
size in determining reasoning performance. Studies 
[14], [15]
 
demonstrated that curated question subsets
—
filtered for clarity 
and reasoning depth
—
can yield higher downstream 
performance than raw large
-
scale data. Smaller curated datasets 
facilitate interpretability and stability, particularly in multi
-
agent systems w
here input consistency affects coordination.

Each dimension

reasoning complexity we

relevance to diagnostic and decision

Clinical reasoning complexity (0
–
30 points) is scored 
based on the presence and density of diagnostic or management 
intent indicators, clinical scenario richness, and explicit or 
implicit multi
-
step reasoning. Diagnostic intent indicators 
include phrases s

step,” and “mechanism of action.” Scenario richness is assessed 
by the inclusion of symptoms, physical examination findings, 
family history, and laboratory or imaging results. Multi
-
step 
reasoning is identified 
through causal or inferential language 
(e.g., “because,” “therefore,” “leads to”).
 
Medical knowledge depth (0
–
25 points) evaluates the level 
of biomedical understanding required to answer the question. 
Lower scores correspond to anatomy
-
level or single
-
fact recall, 
while higher scores are assigned to questions involving disease 
mechanism

reasoning with clinical correlation. Questions requiring 
synthesis of knowledge across systems or disease stages receive 
the highest scores.
 
Explanation quality (0
–
20 points) measures the presence 
and depth of explanatory reasoning associated with the correct 
answer. Points are assigned based on explanation length, 
evidence
-
based justification, and explicit reference to 
physiological or patholo
gical mechanisms (e.g., “due to,” 
“mechanism,” “results from”).
 
Option distractibility (0
–
15 points) assesses the quality of 
answer choices. Higher scores are assigned when distractors are 
clinically plausible, comparable in length and terminology 
density, and represent realistic alternative diagnoses. Options 
that are trivial, structurally imbalanced, or semantically 
unrelated receive lower scores.
 
Subject relevance (0
–
10 points) evaluates alignment with 
high
-
yield clinical domains (e.g., cardiology, pulmonology, 
neurology), specificity of the medical topic, and contribution to 
topic diversity within the curated set. Broadly relevant and 
clinically impactful subjects receive higher scores.
 
Each dimension contributes to the final score
 
out of 100
, 
producing a ranked list of questions. 
Experiments varied the 
proportion of high
-

from the top 3% to the top 15% of the 
default

The goal of these five dimensions is to ensure that all selected 
questions provide relevant context to the model. Clinical 
relevance and educational value for the model is prioritized in 
hopes of improving model reasoning and consistency within the 
framework

V.
 
C
ONCLUSION 
AND 
P
ERSPECTIVE
 
Large language models exhibit persistent limitations in 
medical reasoning, including hallucinations and inconsistent 
performance across complex clinical scenarios. This study 
addressed these challenges through a multi
-
agent framework 
integrating Retrieval
-
Augmented Generation (RAG) with 
Model Context Protocol (MCP), enabling five domain
-
specialized agents to coordinate via weighted consensus 
mechanisms.
 
Experimental evaluation on MedMCQA and MedQA
-
USMLE benchmarks demonstrates three critical findings. First, 
multi
-
agent collaboration achieved approximately 5% accuracy 
improvement over single
-
agent baselines (71
-
75% vs. 66
-
70%) 
without fine
-
tuning. Second,
 
ablation studies confirmed that 
consensus mechanisms contributed 1.0
-
2.4% additional gains 
beyond retrieval augmentation alone. Third, and most 
significantly, knowledge quality dramatically outweighs 
quantity: curating only the top 11
-
13% highest
-
quality 
questions yielded ~10% overall improvement, reaching ~80% 
accuracy while reducing computational overhead.
 
These findings establish practical pathways for deploying 
resource
-
efficient medical AI in clinical settings. Future work 
should address four priorities: multimodal data integration 
(imaging, EHR, genomics) to handle real
-
world clinical 
complexity, uncerta
inty
-
aware consensus for safety
-
critical 
decisions, adaptive knowledge curation for dynamic domain 
evolution, and prospective clinical validation to assess real
-
world safety, interpretability, and user acceptance.
 
More broadly, this work demonstrates that strategic 
specialization and quality
-
focused curation outperform scale
-
dependent approaches, providing a practical roadmap for 
developing accurate, transparent, and computationally efficient 
medical AI systems appl
icable across knowledge
-
intensive 
domains
.
