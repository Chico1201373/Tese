Abstract—Accessing global medical knowledge on rare oph-
thalmic diseases remains a challenge for small healthcare centers,
as relevant literature and case reports are scattered across
multiple languages and formats. To bridge this gap, we introduce
ELENA (Eye-disease Language-model for Evidence-based Navi-
gation and Assistance), a retrieval-augmented chatbot designed
to provide multilingual access to specialized medical knowledge
in ophthalmic rare diseases. ELENA combines a quantized Large
Language model with a lightweight retrieval-augmented genera-
tion pipeline, where all source documents are pre-translated into
English to ensure consistent embedding and retrieval. Italian
user queries are automatically translated, processed through
the pipeline, and the generated responses are translated back
into Italian, yielding fluent and contextually grounded answers.
Experiments on a curated corpus of ophthalmic rare-disease
documents confirm the technical feasibility of deploying such
systems even on modest hardware, while a graphical interface
ensures accessibility for non-technical clinical staff. ELENA thus
demonstrates how an efficient, privacy-preserving, and human-
friendly AI assistant can democratize access to international
medical knowledge within local healthcare environments.

I. I NTRODUCTION
In recent years, the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI)
into clinical and research workflows has shown remarkable
potential in supporting medical professionals across a wide
range of specialties. However, within the Italian healthcare
system, small local health authorities (Aziende Sanitarie Lo-
cali, ASL) often face substantial limitations in terms of fi-
nancial resources, technological infrastructure, and specialized
personnel. This issue becomes even more pronounced when
dealing with a case of a rare disease, where data scarcity and
fragmented literature hinder effective diagnosis and research
progress. In the ophthalmic domain, rare diseases have been
estimated to have a prevalence of approximately 6-8%, with
the majority being genetic in origin [1]. According to the Na-
tional Eye Institute, over 900 rare eye diseases are listed, with
one common form, retinitis pigmentosa, affecting an estimated
1 in 5,000 people. On average, it takes 8 years to receive an

accurate diagnosis for a rare disease, and about 95% of them
lack an FDA-approved treatment [2]. In previous work, efforts
were made in this direction to develop intelligent systems for
medical diagnostic support aimed at promptly identifying the
presence of ocular diseases [3]–[5]. In this scenario, it becomes
crucial to streamline the diagnostic process by ensuring that
even small research units and local healthcare institutions
have timely access to the most up-to-date medical knowledge.
To address these issues, this work introduces ELENA (Eye-
disease Language-model for Evidence-based Navigation and
Assistance), a retrieval-augmented conversational framework
specifically designed to assist clinicians and young medical
researchers in exploring rare ophthalmic diseases. The frame-
work combines large language models (LLMs) with retrieval-
augmented generation (RAG) to provide accurate, context-
aware, and human-like responses to user queries posed in
natural Italian language (Fig.1), considering the increasing role
of LLMs in healthcare [6], [7].
ELENA operates by translating Italian user questions into
English, performing semantic retrieval across a multilingual
document repository, and generating coherent answers that are
subsequently translated back into Italian. In this way, even

II. R ELATED W ORKS
The concept of developing a chatbot leveraging a LLM
to provide answers related to ophthalmology field has been
explored in the literature. In [8], a cross-sectional study
compared ChatGPT-generated responses to eye care questions
with answers written by American Academy of Ophthal-
mology–affiliated ophthalmologists. Using 200 deidentified
patient questions from an online forum, a panel of board-
certified ophthalmologists assessed whether answers were AI-
or human-generated, and evaluated accuracy, appropriateness,
and potential for harm. The study found that ChatGPT re-
sponses were largely comparable to human-written answers
in terms of correctness, likelihood of harm, and alignment
with community standards. Considering solutions or studies
that have focused on specific disorders, [9] and [10]. The
first work evaluated an LLM chatbot (GPT-4) in ophthal-
mology, comparing its diagnostic accuracy and completeness
to fellowship-trained glaucoma and retina specialists using
both commonly asked questions and real patient cases. For
glaucoma-related scenarios, the chatbot achieved higher mean
ranks for accuracy and completeness compared with special-
ists. For retina-related scenarios, the chatbot matched special-
ists in accuracy and outperformed them in completeness. The
second work introduced CataractBot, an LLM-based system
designed to answer cataract surgery–related questions using
a curated, expert-verified knowledge base. Deployed in a
real-world study with patients, doctors, and coordinators, the
bot demonstrated potential for providing instant, accessible,
and privacy-preserving information. It supported multilingual
and multimodal interactions, accommodated varying literacy
levels, and helped mitigate power imbalances. However, an
issue that emerges from the analysis of the state of the art
is the near absence of studies addressing the domain of rare
diseases. As discussed in [11], even GPT-4 shows poorer
performance in the context of rare diseases. Furthermore, the
literature includes studies proposing generic solutions for rare
diseases in general, as in the case of [12], or, as observed in the
works reviewed, focusing on a single type of disease within
a specific domain. The proposed ELENA framework uniquely
combines RAG with multilingual translation to support small
Italian healthcare institutions, enabling questions posed in
Italian to access a curated global knowledge base of rare
eye disease documents. Unlike large, general-purpose LLMs,
which may have broader but less domain-focused knowledge,
ELENA relies exclusively on selected and regularly updated
documents (also internal, considering the federated institution-
oriented nature of the framework [13]). This strategy enhances
domain-specific expertise while covering the full spectrum
of rare eye diseases, rather than concentrating on a single
condition, thereby ensuring both depth and breadth in the
clinical knowledge accessible to users.
III. M ETHODOLOGY
The ELENA framework was designed to provide an effec-
tive multilingual conversational assistant for rare ophthalmic
diseases within local Italian healthcare institutions. The sys-
tem addresses one of the major challenges in real-world
medical information retrieval: the coexistence of heteroge-
neous textual sources written in multiple languages. To ensure
a unified semantic space for knowledge access, all source
documents—initially collected in Italian, English, and other
languages are pre-processed and automatically translated into
English using a neural machine translation model. This step
allows ELENA to operate within a consistent embedding
space, facilitating accurate retrieval regardless of the docu-
ment’s original language. When a user poses a question in
Italian, the query is first translated into English, embedded, and
compared against the pre-processed corpus to retrieve the most
semantically relevant passages. These retrieved segments form
the contextual basis for the subsequent RAG phase, in which
the LLM synthesizes an informed and human-like answer in
English. Finally, the generated answer is translated back into
Italian and presented to the user in a fluent, natural-language
format. This multilingual pipeline ensures both linguistic ac-
cessibility for Italian healthcare professionals and semantic
fidelity to the underlying clinical knowledge base, making
ELENA a practical and privacy-preserving conversational tool
for small-scale medical institutions.
A. Datasets
For the ophthalmology-focused implementation of the
ELENA framework, a domain-specific dataset was constructed
to evaluate the system’s capacity to retrieve and synthesize
information on rare eye diseases. In this conceptual stage of
the frame, the dataset consists of a collection of plain-text
(.txt) files, each dedicated to a distinct condition, including but
not limited to: Retinitis pigmentosa, Leber congenital amau-
rosis, Usher syndrome, Juvenile macular degeneration, Leber
hereditary optic neuropathy, Congenital optic atrophy, Fuchs’
endothelial dystrophy, Hereditary neurotrophic keratitis, Reis-
B¨ucklers corneal dystrophy, Congenital hereditary cataract,
Microspherophakia, Marfan syndrome (ocular manifestations),
Lowe syndrome, Alport syndrome, Parry-Romberg syndrome,
Congenital aniridia, and Axenfeld-Rieger syndrome. Each
document contains descriptive medical knowledge about the
associated pathology, encompassing aspects such as clinical
presentation, genetic etiology, diagnostic markers, and man-
agement approaches. While the current experimental setup
relies on manually curated text files to ensure data consistency
and traceability, the framework is agnostic to input format. In
real-world deployments, ELENA could seamlessly incorporate

the feasibility and precision of retrieval-augmented reason-

foundation for future scaling to institutional and multilingual
data collections. Furthermore, to assess the validity of the
generated outputs, the framework was trained and evaluated
using the ReDis dataset (https://huggingface.co/datasets/guan-
wang/ReDis-QA), which comprises 1,360 multiple-choice
questions covering 205 distinct rare diseases. The questions are
annotated according to disease properties such as symptoms,
causes, effects, related disorders, and diagnosis, providing a
structured framework for model assessment. However, the
dataset exhibits some limitations: its modest size may be
insufficient for training complex models from scratch; the
coverage, although spanning 205 diseases, leaves many rare
conditions unrepresented; and the multiple-choice format re-
stricts generative, free-form QA capabilities. These limitations
highlight the importance of complementing static datasets with
dynamic retrieval from up-to-date scientific publications and
official medical resources, ensuring that AI models can access
the most recent and comprehensive knowledge without being
constrained by a unique and, potentially outdated, dataset.
However, the use of this dataset was crucial, owing to the
presence of reference answers, which made possible to quan-
titatively evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed pipeline
by measuring how closely the model’s generated responses
aligned with the medically expected answers.
B. Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Response Pipeline
In this study, we utilized Falcon-7B-Instruct [14], a 7-
billion parameter instruction-tuned LLM designed for natural
language understanding and generation. Falcon-7B-Instruct is
based on the Falcon architecture, which has demonstrated
strong performance in instruction-following tasks, while re-
maining computationally efficient compared to other models
of similar scale. To enable deployment on hardware with lim-
ited memory, we employed 4-bit quantization which allowed
the Falcon-7B-Instruct model to run efficiently on standard
GPUs while maintaining high-quality generative capabilities,
mimicking the modest hardware availability that would charac-
terize a real deployment scenario. The modular design of the
framework ensures that the underlying language model can
be substituted with more advanced or larger-scale alternatives
as these emerge, without necessitating major modifications to
the retrieval or response-generation components. To enhance
the accuracy and relevance of the model’s responses, we
implemented a RAG system. In this framework, the language
model does not rely solely on its pre-trained knowledge but
can access to external, updated documents on eye rare disease
domain. Since the corpus consists of documents written in
multiple languages, all texts were first translated into English
to create a unified semantic space for retrieval and generation.
To facilitate efficient retrieval, the input documents were
segmented into smaller, semantically coherent chunks using
aRecursiveCharacterTextSplitter with a chunk size of 800
characters and an overlap of 100. Each chunk was then em-
bedded using the compact all-MiniLM-L6-v2 [15] model from
the Sentence-Transformers family, which provides lightweight
yet semantically rich vector representations. The embeddings
were stored in a vector database to enable similarity-based
retrieval through approximate nearest neighbor search. When
a user poses a question in Italian, the system first translates it
into English, then it is used to retrieve the top-k most relevant
document chunks. The retrieved information is concatenated
into a contextual prompt and passed to the instruction-tuned
LLM model to generate a coherent and medically accurate
response in English. The generation prompt explicitly guides
the model to produce a concise, human-like explanation of the
queried disease or concept, summarizing key clinical aspects
in three to five sentences. The prompt used is proposed in

This architecture thus bridges linguistic and computational
gaps by combining cross-lingual translation, dense semantic
retrieval, and instruction-based text generation, providing a
scalable and privacy-preserving assistant for rare ophthalmo-
logic disease management.
IV. R ESULTS
All experiments were conducted on Google Colab using a
T4 GPU (15 GB RAM), simulating the limited computational
resources typical of possible real-world clinical settings. De-
spite the relatively modest hardware resources, the use of 4-bit
quantization allowed us to load and run the Falcon-7B-Instruct
model efficiently, significantly reducing memory requirements
without substantial loss in performance.
This setup enabled the execution of both the RAG pipeline
and the LLM inference in a single environment. The system
was able to process questions, perform semantic retrieval
from the knowledge base, generate responses in English, and
translate them back into Italian in real time. Given that the
current study is primarily conceptual, the documents used for
preliminary human testing of the framework were stored and
accessed locally. The quality of the generated answers was
evaluated using multiple metrics, including cosine similarity
of embeddings, cosine similarity of TF-IDF vectors, BLEU,
ROUGE-L, Levenshtein distance, and an aggregated similarity
score. The results, summarized in Table II, show that the
framework produces contextually relevant and semantically
coherent answers, despite the limited computational resources.
Examining the results numerically, we can observe that the
average values of the metrics are relatively low: the mean
cosine similarity of embeddings is 0.379, the cosine similarity
based on TF-IDF is 0.122, BLEU is 0.002, ROUGE-L is
0.036, and Levenshtein distance normalized similarity is 0.048.

V. C ONCLUSION AND F UTURE D IRECTIONS
This study presented ELENA, a retrieval-augmented con-
versational framework designed to support clinicians and
researchers in the ophthalmological rare disease domain.
The results show that a modular hybrid approach-combining
translation-based retrieval with an instruction-tuned LLM can
generate medically coherent, context-aware answers within a
lightweight, privacy-preserving setup suitable for small Ital-
ian healthcare institutions. Several important directions for
future work must be taken in consideration. First, while we
have demonstrated the feasibility of the framework, only a
limited number of models and documents were tested, and
the evaluation remains preliminary. A more comprehensive
human evaluation—ideally conducted with young clinicians
and researchers from small ASL centers will be essential
to assess clinical relevance, readability and explainability,
as previously demonstrated in [16] and [17]. Second, the
dynamic knowledge base, continuously updated, should in-
corporate, ideally via secure ingest pipelines, versioning and
audit-logging that respect patient privacy and institutional
governance. Finally, to foster long-term adoption in small
healthcare institutions, future work should focus on usability
and integration: enhancing the graphical interface, deploying
