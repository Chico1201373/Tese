MediTriR: A Triple-Driven Approach to
Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Medical
Question Answering Tasks
Hongzhi Zhang, M. Omair Shafiq
School of Information Technology
Carleton University
Ottawa, ON, Canada
Emails: hongzhizhang@cmail.carleton.ca, omair.shafiq@carleton.ca
Abstract—Advances in large language models have driven
progress in medical question-answering systems, but challenges
remain in accuracy and relevance, especially in complex medical
settings. To address this problem, we introduce MediTriR. This
approach combines knowledge graph triples with a retrieval-
augmented generation framework to enhance the performance
of large language models in medical multiple-choice question-
answering tasks. Our approach transforms the retrieved medical
knowledge into structured triples and uses these triples for accu-
rate information retrieval. Furthermore, we dynamically generate
new triples based on the properties of triples to enrich the
model’s reasoning ability. The integration of retrieval-augmented
generation enables more accurate and contextual answers by
leveraging external medical triples. MediTriR is evaluated against
pure large language model approaches using the MedMCQA and
Medical QA datasets, showing better performance in terms of
accuracy and relevance. These results highlight the potential of
our approach to improve medical decision support and education.

Model, Retrieval-augmented generation, Knowledge Graphs,

I. I NTRODUCTION
In recent years, there has been rapid progress in the appli-
cation of large language models (LLMs) in natural language
processing. These models, such as ChatGPT [1], have demon-
strated remarkable capabilities in understanding and gener-
ating human-like text. In the medical field, applying LLMs
to question-answering tasks opens up new possibilities for
assisting medical professionals and patients. One specific area
of interest is multiple-choice questions (MCQ) in the medical
domain, where the goal is to accurately select the correct
answer from a set of options based on medical literature.
However, LLMs can hallucinate [2] and generate incor-
rect answers, which is a critical issue in some fields, such
as medicine. Furthermore, data in the pre-training phase
of LLM often lacks the depth required for specialized do-
main question-answering tasks. To address these limitations,
retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) [3] techniques have
been developed. RAG enhances LLM performance by combin-
ing retrieval and generation processes: it first retrieves relevant
information from a large document set or database and then
uses this data to inform the generation phase where the model
creates responses. This integration helps improve the accuracy
and relevance of answers, making RAG particularly valuable
in complex fields such as medicine.
Despite the progress of LLMs in medical tasks, there are
still some challenges in using LLMs for medical MCQ tasks.
The main problem is to efficiently retrieve and integrate
relevant information from large biomedical datasets to answer
questions accurately. Traditional retrieval methods sometimes
fail to capture the subtle and complex relationships in med-
ical texts, resulting in incomplete or incorrect answers. This
problem is worth studying because improving the accuracy
and reliability of medical question-answering systems will
have a direct impact on medical decision-making and patient
outcomes.
Our research aims to develop a method, MediTriR, to
improve the performance of LLM in medical MCQ tasks
by leveraging knowledge graph triples. Knowledge graphs
represent information in a structured format that represents the
relationships between entities. By converting medical literature
into knowledge graph triples and integrating them into the
question-answering process, we aim to improve the model’s
ability to retrieve and reason about relevant information. An
example of our method is shown in Fig. 1.
The contributions in this paper are as follows:
• We propose a triple-based RAG method to solve the
multiple-choice question-answering task in the medical
field. Replacing text with triples allows LLMs to obtain
more effective information.
• We experimented with multiple models on multiple
datasets and performed ablation experiments to demon-
strate the effectiveness of our proposed method.
• We analyze each step of our method in detail and explain
how these steps help LLMs solve MCQs in the medical
field.

III. S YSTEM DESIGN
A. Overview of the Approach
Our approach combines structured knowledge with the
generative power of LLMs. The system aims to improve the
accuracy and relevance of answers generated by the model,
ensuring that they are based on reliable medical literature.
The process begins by generating PubMed [19] queries based
on medical questions. These queries are designed to retrieve
articles that contain relevant information needed to answer
the questions. We then searched and retrieved paper abstracts
from the PubMed database using the Entrez API [19]. In this
step, we obtained rich medical information, which provided
a knowledge base for the RAG process. After retrieving the
abstracts, we converted them into triples in the knowledge
graph. Each triple represents a piece of information in the
format of (subject, predicate, object), which captures the
basic relations and entities described in the abstract. This
structured representation allows for more efficient and accurate
processing of information. The next step involves evaluating
the relevance of these triples to the medical question. This
filtering process ensures that only relevant triples are retained,
reducing noise and focusing the model’s attention on the most
relevant data. Next, we generate new triples based on the
existing triples. This step exploits the extensibility of triples to
infer additional useful information that may not be explicitly
stated in the original abstract. Finally, the system combines
the retained triples and the newly generated triples to answer
the medical MCQ. The framework of our method is shown in
Fig. 2.
B. Generating PubMed Queries
Inspired by [4], the first step in our approach involves
transforming medical questions into PubMed search queries
and retrieving relevant paper abstracts. In this step, we col-
lected comprehensive biomedical documents to provide the
basic information required for subsequent processing. We used
the LLM, GPT-4o [20] to generate effective PubMed queries
from medical questions. The main goal is to create queries
to retrieve articles containing relevant information. For each
query, we retrieved 15 papers. Such a retrieval method can
maximize capturing all relevant information required to answer
the question accurately. An example is shown in Fig. 3.
C. Retrieving Paper Abstracts
After generating a PubMed query, the next step is to use
the Entrez API to search and retrieve relevant article abstracts
from the PubMed database. The search process is performed
as follows:
• Search PubMed Database: Use the generated query to
search the PubMed database via the Entrez API. The
search is configured to retrieve 15 relevant articles.
• Retrieve Article IDs: Extract the unique identifiers (IDs)
of the retrieved articles from the search results.
• Fetch Abstracts: Use the article IDs to fetch the corre-
sponding paper abstracts from PubMed.
D. Converting Abstracts into Triples
After retrieving the relevant paper abstracts, the next step in-
volves converting these abstracts into knowledge graph triples
and assessing their relevance to the medical question.
The conversion of paper abstracts into knowledge graph
triples using an LLM, GPT-4o [20]. Each triple captures
the core relationships and entities described in the abstract.
This structured representation allows for the more efficient
processing and reasoning of biomedical information.
We instruct an LLM, GPT-4o [20], to act as an assistant
that converts abstracts into triples. The model is given the
task of reading the abstracts and extracting information in
the form of triples. For instance, an abstract describing a
medical trial might be converted into triples like (Drug A,
Treats, Disease B) or (Disease B, Associated With, Symptom
C). These triples encapsulate the key pieces of information in
a structured format that can be easily queried and analyzed.
The prompt is shown in Fig. 4.
E. Assessing Relevance of Triples
Once the triples are generated, the next step is to filter them,
keeping only those relevant to the medical question. This step
ensures that the model focuses on the most relevant informa-
tion, thereby reducing noise and improving the accuracy of
the answer.
Relevance assessment is performed by instructing the LLM
to act as a medical researcher, evaluating whether each triple
contains information that may be relevant to the medical
question. The model reviews each triple in the context of the
question and keeps only those considered relevant. For exam-
ple, if the medical question is about the treatment of a specific
disease, only triples related to the treatment, medication, or
medical outcome of that disease will be kept.
By combining these steps, we ensure that our system has a
structured and filtered knowledge graph that is triple-relevant
to the medical question.

F . Generating New Medical Triples Based on Existing Triples
After converting the abstracts into knowledge graph triples
and filtering for relevance, the next step is to use the LLM to
generate new medical triples based on existing triples. This
step exploits the extensibility of triples to infer additional
useful information that may not be explicitly stated in the
original abstract.
We use the LLM to generate new triples. The goal is to
create new accurate triples by logically combining or inferring
information from a given set of existing triples. This process
helps to expand the knowledge base and discover implicit
relationships that are not directly mentioned in the retrieved
abstracts but are valuable for answering medical questions.
We instruct the LLM to act as an assistant to generate new
triples from a list of existing triples. The model’s task is to
review existing triples and create new triples that follow the
same format. At the same time, we also pass in the original
medical question as a reference for the LLM when generating
new triples. These new triples should be meaningful and error-
free, ensuring that they make positive contributions to the
knowledge base.
For example, if the existing triples include (drug A, treat-
ment, disease B) and (disease B, related, symptom C), the
model may generate a new triple such as (drug A, reduction,
symptom C). This new triple follows logically from existing
information and provides additional insights that may be
valuable in answering medical questions. The prompt is shown
in Fig. 5.

V. D ISCUSSION
In this chapter, we discuss the advantages of our exper-
imental approach to the medical multiple-choice question-
answering task. Our approach leverages the RAG approach and
is enhanced using knowledge graph triples. This innovative
combination provides multiple benefits that can improve the
accuracy and reliability of medical question-answering sys-
tems.
A. Benefits of the RAG Method for Medical Problems
The first step in our approach involves converting medical
questions into search queries suitable for retrieving relevant
medical literature. This is accomplished using an LLM specifi-
cally instructed to generate PubMed queries based on the given
questions.
By converting medical questions into well-formulated
search queries, we can target the retrieval of the most relevant
documents from PubMed. This ensures that the retrieved
information is closely aligned with the specific needs of
the question, reducing the likelihood of irrelevant data being
included in the subsequent steps.
Once the queries are generated, the next step involves using
the Entrez API to search for paper abstracts in PubMed. The
advantages of this approach include:
• PubMed is one of the most extensive medical literature
databases, containing millions of abstracts from peer-
reviewed journals. By leveraging PubMed, we ensure that
the information retrieved comes from authoritative and
recent research articles, which is critical to maintaining
accuracy and trust in answers.
• The Entrez API allows precise control of search param-
eters, thereby retrieving the documents most relevant to
the query. This targeted retrieval reduces noise in the data
and ensures that subsequent steps in our approach use the
highest quality information.
• The retrieved abstracts provide a rich source of informa-
tion that LLM can use to generate accurate answers. By
combining this external data with the model’s existing
knowledge, we enhance the model’s ability to effectively
answer complex medical questions.
The strength of the RAG approach is its ability to combine
retrieving information and generating answers. A common
problem with generative models is hallucinations, producing
information that appears plausible but is incorrect. The RAG
approach reduces the risk of hallucinations by first retrieving
relevant documents and then using those documents to gen-
erate answers. This combination ensures that answers are not
only coherent and contextual but also grounded in validated
medical literature.
B. Benefits of Converting Text Data into Triples
1) Enhanced Semantic Information: Triples provide a clear
and structured representation of the information contained in
the abstract. Such a structured format makes it easier for the
LLM to process and understand the relationships between
different medical concepts. By breaking down complex sen-
tences into simpler, more understandable units, the LLM can
effectively use this information to generate accurate answers.
By converting abstracts into triples, we extract the most
relevant information from medical papers. This extraction pro-
cess filters out irrelevant details, allowing the model to focus
on key facts and relationships directly related to the medical
question. This focus enhances the relevance and quality of
the information used by the model, resulting in more accurate
answers.
2) Accurate Relevance Assessment: By evaluating the rel-
evance of triples, we try to ensure that LLM uses only the
most relevant information in the answer-generation process.
Precision knowledge and information are very important in the
medical field, as accurate and reliable information is important
for patient care. By filtering out irrelevant triples, we reduce
the risk of incorporating misleading or irrelevant information
into the answer.
Evaluating the relevance of triples simplifies the data pro-
cessing workflow. By focusing only on relevant triples, we can
minimize the computational resources required for subsequent
steps. This efficiency also helps in real-time applications that
require fast and accurate responses.
3) Scalability introduces more knowledge: Generating new
triples allows us to expand the knowledge base beyond the
information initially retrieved. By logically combining existing
triples, the model can generate new, meaningful triples that
provide additional insights. For example, if we have a triple
stating that drug A can treat disease B and another triple stating
that disease B is associated with symptom D, the model may
generate a new triple stating that drug A can alleviate symptom
D. This expansion based on the unique properties of triples can
bring more valuable insights to the medical field.
Generating new triples enhances the model’s ability to
provide accurate and detailed answers. By supplementing
the original information with inferred data, the model can
combine knowledge from multiple papers to provide more
comprehensive answers and address the nuances of medical
questions. This capability is particularly useful in complex
medical scenarios where multiple factors need to be consid-
ered.
Not all relevant information is always explicitly stated in the
medical literature. By generating new triples, the model can
fill in the gaps in the existing knowledge base, ensuring that
all necessary information is available to answer the question.
This proactive approach mitigates the limitations of incomplete
data and improves the overall robustness of the system.
VI. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose an innovative approach, MediTriR,
to improve the accuracy and relevance of answers provided

by LLMs in medical multiple-choice tasks. Our approach
combines the strengths of the RAG framework with a novel
triple-based data representation and shows great potential in
improving the quality of medical multiple-choice tasks.
Our approach first uses LLM to convert medical questions
into precise PubMed search queries, ensuring the retrieval of
highly relevant medical literature. This initial step leverages
PubMed’s extensive resources and the precision of the Entrez
API, which together facilitate the extraction of authoritative
and up-to-date information. This process addresses the need
for reliable and relevant data sources.
After retrieving paper abstracts, we convert these abstracts
into structured triples, which provide a clear and manageable
format for representing complex medical information. This
step enhances the clarity and relevance of the data, allowing
the LLM to focus on the essential relationships between
medical concepts. By evaluating the relevance of these triples
to the medical question, we ensure that only the most relevant
information is used, further improving the accuracy of the
generated answers.
One key innovation of our approach is generating new
triples based on existing triples. This feature allows the system
to expand its knowledge base and infer more relevant informa-
tion, thereby filling gaps and improving the comprehensiveness
of answers. Such innovations can be helpful in medical tasks,
as the medical field often requires complex reasoning and
comprehensive knowledge.
Our approach can reduce the risk of hallucination problems
common in generative models by basing the generation process
on retrieved, verified information. This foundation ensures that
the answers are coherent, contextual, and based on reliable
medical literature.
Although our approach has shown promising results, several
