Abstract—Traditional question-answering (QA) systems in
multilingual classrooms struggle when handling Arabic, French,
and English instructions alongside complex mathematical nota-
tion in Moroccan curricula. This study introduces an advanced
trilingual Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework for
curriculum-based QA across these three languages. The proposed
approach combines specialized document parsing with right-
to-left text handling, staged evaluation of Naive, Hybrid, and
Advanced RAG models, and advanced techniques such as query
expansion, rank fusion, reranking, and confidence scoring to re-
duce LLM hallucinations. Using dual retrieval (dense and sparse),
multi-stage ranking, multilingual embeddings, and DeepSeek
R1 for generation, the Advanced pipeline achieves near-perfect
embedding similarity (0.95+ correctness, 0.93+ faithfulness) and
strong trilingual performance, with Arabic queries reaching
French-level accuracy. The results highlight a practical, context-
aware solution for multilingual educational QA. Future work will
explore Agentic RAG architectures to address reasoning gaps and
multi-turn dialogue.
Index Terms —Large Language Models, Retrieval-Augmented
Generation, Multilingual Question Answering, Hybrid retrieval,
Rank fusion, Cross-encoder re-ranking, Embedding scoring.
I. I NTRODUCTION
The educational landscape in multilingual contexts presents
unique challenges for artificial intelligence systems, especially
in countries like Morocco where instruction occurs across
Arabic, French, and English (and occasionally Spanish)
within the same curriculum framework. Traditional question-
answering systems struggle to correctly handle this diversity,
specifically the complex mathematical notation and right-
to-left (RTL) text processing required for comprehensive
educational support [1, 19].
Recent advances in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
have shown promise for knowledge-intensive educational
applications [13]. However, existing implementations remain
predominantly monolingual and fail to address the specific
requirements of multilingual educational content processing.
Morocco’s educational system further exacerbates these
challenges, with approximately 68% of secondary education
conducted in Arabic, 28% in French, and a growing 4% in
English for technical subjects [17].
Mathematical and scientific curricula frequently contain
complex formulas, diagrams, and specialized notation that
require precise preservation during document processing.
Current educational QA systems demonstrate significant
performance degradation when handling Arabic mathematical
content, with parsing accuracy dropping to 52% compared
to 89% for equivalent English documents [16] which is
a limitation directly addressed by the parser developed
in this study. Furthermore, existing multilingual RAG
implementations exhibit substantial language bias, with
Arabic queries receiving 34% lower retrieval accuracy than
English equivalents in cross-lingual scenarios [5].
The preservation of mathematical formulas in educational
content presents a critical challenge for RAG systems,
particularly for the scientific specialization in Morocco.
Studies indicate that up to 73% of mathematical expressions
are corrupted or lost during traditional PDF parsing processes
[24], limiting the utility of educational QA systems for STEM
subjects. This problem is compounded in Arabic educational
documents, where the combination of RTL text flow and
embedded mathematical notation creates additional parsing
complexities that existing systems fail to address adequately
[10].

primary research questions:

II. LITERATUREREVIEW
A. RAG systems
Recent literature demonstrates how RAG has evolved from
simple question-answering systems to advanced hybrid models
[18]. The basic RAG system integrates neural retrievers with
sequence-to-sequence generators to connect results with ex-
ternal documents, achieving high performance on knowledge-
based tasks. Modern RAG systems utilize modular designs
that include chunking, embedding, retrieval, and generation
components, acting as ”live memory” for large language mod-
els [29]. However, traditional dense retrieval methods struggle
with specific terminology and exact keyword matching, neces-
sitating hybrid retrieval strategies that mix sparse and dense
methods; a strategy integrated into our Hybrid architecture.
B. Multilingual Information Retrieval and RAG
Cross-lingual RAG presents significant performance gaps
across languages [5]. Recent methods show that while re-
sponse language correctness is a hurdle in monolingual re-
trieval settings, the primary challenge in multilingual retrieval
remains reasoning across languages rather than generation
quality [14]. Culturally informed multilingual RAG research
indicates that contextualizing with views across multiple
languages increases robustness and decreases geopolitical
bias, with multilingual document retrieval outperforming in-
language-only strategies [12]. Evaluation paradigms exhibit
uneven linguistic distribution in test suite outputs, revealing
a strong need for purpose-built multilingual architectures.
C. Educational AI and Curriculum-Based QA Systems
Educational RAG applications have demonstrated promis-
ing results in providing personalized learning support with
high availability [2]. Recent implementations employ adaptive
questioning mechanisms that adjust explanations based on
student proficiency. Enhanced question-answering systems for
skill-based learning integrate knowledge-based AI frameworks
with generative models to provide explanations encompass-
ing both procedural knowledge and reasoning [6]. However,
curriculum-aware systems face challenges in processing com-
plex mathematical notation and maintaining context across
multi-turn dialogues.
D. Hybrid Retrieval and Ranking Fusion
Hybrid retrieval strategies combining BM25 sparse re-
trieval with dense embedding methods consistently outperform
single-method approaches [9]. Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)
has emerged as an effective technique for merging ranked lists,
with empirical studies showing approximately 20.4% relative
gains over dense retrieval alone and 9.54% over BM25 in
out-of-domain benchmarks. Cross-encoder reranking provides
further improvements by processing query-document pairs
jointly, enabling more sophisticated relevance scoring than bi-
encoder approaches [22].
E. Document Parsing and Mathematical Content Extraction
Extracting mathematical formulas from PDF documents re-
mains a significant challenge. Conventional parsing techniques
achieve only 52% accuracy on sophisticated mathematical ma-
terial versus 89% for ordinary text [23]. Recent developments
use modular pipelines including layout detection, OCR, and
expression extraction. Specialized models perform better on
diverse document types [4]. While tools like PDF-Extract-
Kit [25] exhibit strong performance, the intricacy of fusing
embedded mathematical notation with RTL text flow makes
Arabic mathematical content analysis particularly difficult,
necessitating the specific pretreatment methods proposed in
this study.
III. METHODOLOGY
This section presents our progressive approach to devel-
oping a robust trilingual RAG framework. Figure 1 depicts
the proposed Advanced Pipeline Architecture, illustrating the
integration of specialized document parsing, dual retrieval
mechanisms (sparse and dense), multi-stage ranking with
cross-encoder reranking, query expansion via LLM-based
paraphrasing and Pseudo-Relevance Feedback (PRF), diversity
promotion through Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR), and
confidence-aware generation.

Fig. 1. Advanced Pipeline Architecture Flow
The architecture operates through several key stages: follow-
ing document parsing and chunking, user queries undergo ex-
pansion and encoding into semantic vectors. The system then
performs parallel sparse (BM25) and dense (FAISS) retrieval,
fuses results via RRF, applies MMR-based diversification,
performs cross-encoder reranking, and generates confidence-
scored answers using a quantized LLM with language-adaptive
prompting. This end-to-end pipeline was developed progres-
sively through three iteration: Naive, Hybrid, and Advanced.
A. Progressive Pipeline Development
To systematically evaluate the contribution of each archi-
tectural component, we developed three progressively sophis-
ticated RAG pipelines.
1) Naive RAG Pipeline (Baseline):The baseline imple-
ments standard dense retrieval using multilingual embeddings
(multilingual-e5-large [26]), FAISS vector search [8], and a
quantized LLM (DeepSeek/Qwen3-8B) [28] for generation.
Documents are segmented into overlapping, sentence-based
chunks with language-adaptive prompts to establish baseline
metrics.
2) Hybrid RAG Pipeline:The Hybrid pipeline combines
BM25 [21] sparse retrieval with dense embeddings, merging
results via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF). Top candidates
undergo cross-encoder reranking (ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2
[20]) for improved relevance scoring, balancing speed and
accuracy.
3) Advanced Hybrid RAG Pipeline:Building upon the
Hybrid foundation, the Advanced pipeline (Figure 1) incor-
porates query expansion, PRF, MMR-based diversification
with source deduplication, and confidence scoring to detect
hallucinations. This multi-stage architecture delivers robust
answers optimized for multilingual educational content.
IV. EXPERIMENTALSETUP
A. Dataset Construction and Preparation
The experimental framework evaluates multilingual retrieval
and reasoning over Moroccan educational resources. The
dataset comprises approximately twenty documents spanning
mathematics, life sciences (SVT), Arabic language, history,
geography, and English, sourced from public curriculum
repositories. Materials were provided in PDF and DOCX
formats across Arabic, French, and English.
Document parsing employed a dual-strategy approach
based on language and content type: LlamaParse[15] with
scientific presets for French and English documents, and
PyMuPDF[3] augmented witharabic_reshaper[7] and
bidi[11] for Arabic content. This strategy was adopted after
observing that LlamaParse frequently failed to retain Arabic
text and notation, while PyMuPDF proved more reliable for
such content. The system automatically routes documents
to the appropriate parser while preserving mathematical
expressions using LATEX conventions.
For pipeline evaluation, we constructed a JSON dataset
containing structured query-answer pairs. Each entry includes
question ID, source file, question text, gold answer, gold con-
text, and language identifier. Figure 2 illustrates the language
distribution. Cross-lingual testing was performed to assess
retrieval robustness when query language differed from source
document language.
Fig. 2. Language Distribution in the Dataset
After pipeline execution, model outputs were appended
to the JSON structure. The Advanced pipeline additionally
outputs confidence scores derived from cross-encoder rerank-
ing[20], which correlate strongly with answer correctness and
faithfulness.

B. Implementation Details
Experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX
A4000 GPU (16GB VRAM) with 45GB system RAM.
Quantization was applied across all models to optimize
memory usage. The software stack integrated Python
with HuggingFace Transformers[27], FAISS[8], PyMuPDF[3],
LlamaParse[15], BM25[21], and SentenceTransformers[20].
The retrieval pipeline employed dense embeddings from
intfloat/multilingual-e5-large[26], cross-encoder reranking
viams-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2[20], andDeepSeek-R1-8B
for generation. Index construction used FAISS IVF[8] with
empirically selected parameters (48 clusters, 10 probes) and
normalized dense vectors.
C. Evaluation Framework
Evaluation relied on embedding-based similarity to assess
generated text and context alignment with gold standards. The
LLM-judge component was excluded due to hardware con-
straints. Cosine similarity was computed usingmultilingual-
e5-large[26]. Multilingual robustness was assessed by prompt-
ing the LLM in different languages, and mathematical accu-
racy was verified manually. Efficiency metrics included GPU
memory usage and query latency, revealing a trade-off between
accuracy and processing time.
The primary evaluation metrics are:
•embedding_correctness:Cosine similarity
between embeddings ofmodel_answerand
gold_answer.
•embedding_faithfulness:Cosine similarity be-
tweenmodel_answerandretrieved_context.
•correctness_score:LLM-based factual correct-
ness evaluation (0–5 scale).
•faithfulness_score:LLM-based factual ground-
ing evaluation (0–5 scale).
•clarity_score:LLM-based evaluation of answer
clarity (0–5 scale).
V. RESULTS ANDANALYSIS
A. Document Parsing Performance
We evaluated parsing by processing documents through
both parsers and retaining the optimal output. LlamaParse
(scientific preset) excelled on French and English scientific
documents; retaining headings, tables, and display math. How-
ever, it has struggled with Arabic RTL loss. PyMuPDF was
highly efficient and reliable for non-scientific and math-light
documents; with RTL reshaping, it preserved Arabic text flow
but showed limitations with complex formulas. Regarding
efficiency, PyMuPDF was near-instant, while LlamaParse av-
eraged 4-5 minutes per document.

VI. DISCUSSION, IMPLICATIONS ANDLIMITATIONS
The proposedAdvanced Hybrid RAG pipelinedemon-
strates a highly effective architecture for curriculum-based
question answering. Its workflow utilizes curriculum-based
chunking, dual retrieval (Sparse BM25 and Dense FAISS),
query expansion, rank fusion (RRF), cross-encoder reranking,
and confidence scoring to minimize hallucinations.
Impact and performance analysis indicates:
•High Embedding Correctness and Faithfulness:The
Advanced pipeline achieved near-perfect scores, outper-
forming baselines.
•Robust Multilingual Performance:The system main-
tained strong results across Arabic, French, and English.
•State-of-the-Art Design:Integration of scalable re-
trieval, diversification, and confidence scoring establishes
a hallucination-resistant framework.
While the pipeline demonstrates strong performance, lim-
itations remain. First, experiments were conducted on local
hardware, imposing constraints on speed and context windows;
retrieval and generation occasionally required significant pro-
cessing time per run. Second, the availability of high-quality,
standardized Moroccan educational materials is significantly
limited; reliance on teacher contributions introduces variability
in content quality. Future improvements will focus on optimiz-
ing retrieval speed, expanding the dataset with standardized
resources, and integrating larger models while retaining the
reasoning capabilities of DeepSeek.
VII. CONCLUSION
This research has developed an advanced, reliable trilin-
gual RAG framework specifically designed for multilingual
educational environments. Addressing the complexities of the
Moroccan context (where instruction transitions between Ara-
bic, French, and English with technical notation) our system
overcomes the failures of traditional solutions. By engineering
a specialized document parsing pipeline and a sophisticated
RAG architecture with query expansion and confidence es-
timation, we achieved near-perfect accuracy across all three
languages. Notably, performance on Arabic queries rivaled
French and occasionally surpassed English. These findings
represent a significant step toward trustworthy educational sup-
port tools. Future development will focus on query expansion
and context pruning to evolve this framework into a scalable
product for Moroccan schools and broader multilingual con-
texts.
