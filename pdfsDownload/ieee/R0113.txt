Environments: Integrating Retrieval-Augmented

Abstract— We present a customized A rtificial Intelligence 
(AI) chatbot tailored for educational environments. Unlike 
conventional models that rely solely on pretrained knowledge, 
our system integrates rule -based keyword matching and 
retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with internal 
educational resources to produce a ccurate, contextually 
relevant responses aligned with specific lesson plans. The 
chatbot adapts answers based on user attributes such as age 
group, predefined lesson schemas, and institution -defined 
content. To improve precision, it actively engages users by 
requesting clarification when needed. We deploy a compact 
large language model, Qwen-14B, optimized to run efficiently on 
a local setup with 16GB VRAM. This requires a sophisticated 
workflow to sustain performance and responsiveness. For 
general queries  beyond the internal database, the chatbot 
seamlessly switches to a general -purpose mode, balancing 
flexibility with educational alignment.  
Keywords — Educational Chatbot , Retrieval-Augmented 
Generation, Rule-Based Filtering, Curriculum Alignment 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Advances in large language models (LLMs) have enabled 
powerful tools for natural language processing, retrieval, and 
human-computer interaction  [1], [2] . Chatbots such as 
ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Perplexity, and Grok offer instant 
responses to diverse queries  [3], [4], [5] . Despite their 
versatility, these systems often lack alignment with lesson 
plans, risk bypassing learning processes, and remain costly for 
institutions  [6], [7], [8].  
We propose an educational chatbot integrating rule-based 
keyword filters with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) 
over curated resources, ensuring accuracy, curricular 
alignment, and adaptive responses based on learner attributes. 
The system seeks clarification for vague queries and supports 
differentiated instruction. To avoid commercial LLM costs, 
we adopt Qwen-14B [9], deployable on local hardware with 
16GB VRAM. Through workflow design —task 
decomposition, edge-case handling, prompt tuning —and a 
queuing mechanism for concurrent users, the system achieves 
scalable, low-latency performance. 
II. RELATED WORKS  
Large Language Models in Education LLMs are 
increasingly used in education for tutoring, answering queries, 
and administrative support [10], [11]. Commercial systems 
like ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity, and Grok provide 
general conversational capabilities but often lack curricular 
alignment and may produce pedagogically inconsistent or 
factually incorrect responses. Limitations include 
hallucination [12], [13], outdated knowledge [14], and limited 
transparency or personalization. Retrieval -Augmented 
Generation (RAG) [15] addresses these by integrating 
external knowledge retrieval, improving accuracy, 
adaptability, and curriculum alignment. 
Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Education RAG 
combines retrieval with generation to deliver domain-specific, 
accurate, and interpretable outputs  [16]. It has proven 
effective in curriculum - aligned QA systems across subjects 
[17] and in MOOCs [18]. Educational chatbots like Moodle- 
Bot [10], ChatEd [19], and EduChat [19] extend RAG to 
multi-turn dialogues with instructional strategies, while 
systems like VizChat [20] integrate multimodal analytics. 
These approaches enhance engagement, personalization, and 
learning quality. 
Open-Source Models for Privacy, Cost, and Adaptability 
Closed-source LLMs offer high performance but raise privacy 
and cost concerns. Open -source models such as LLaMA, 
Qwen, and DeepSeek allow local deployment, ensuring data 
control, cost savings, and fine- tuning flexibility [4], [9], [21]. 
Toolkits like Unsloth [22] enable efficient parameter-tuning 
(LoRA, QLoRA) for educational use. 
Our Approach We integrate open-source LLMs with three 
components: (1) rule-based safety and curriculum filters co-
designed with educators, (2) a RAG module retrieving from 
lesson-aligned knowledge bases, and (3) lightweight 
personalization adapting responses to student profiles. This 
unified, modular pipeline ensures privacy, interpretability, 
and pedagogical alignment while supporting on -premises 
deployment with resource-efficient serving backends. 
III. BACKGROUND 
Retrieval-Augmented Generation Retrieval -Augmented 
Generation (RAG) extends large language models (LLMs) by 
incorporating external knowledge retrieval during inference. 
While LLMs such as GPT -3 have demonstrated impressive 
generative capabilities, their dependence on static pretraining 
data limits their ability to provide up -to-date or domain -
specific information. RAG addresses this limitation by 
retrieving relevant context from external sources before 
generating responses, thereby improving factuality, 
adaptability, and reliability. 
Core Components  A standard RAG system comprises the 
following stages: 
• Retrieval: Given a query, the system searches 
external knowledge sources (e.g., document stores, APIs, 
vector databases) to retrieve relevant passages.

System Enhancements To improve performance in real -
world educational and enterprise environments, we introduce 
the following enhancements: 
• Hybrid Retrieval: By combining sparse retrieval 
methods (e.g., BM25) with dense embedding-based retrievers 
(e.g., DPR), our system captures both exact term matches and 
semantic relevance. 
• Contextual Re-Ranking: Retrieved passages are re -
ranked using neural scoring models to prioritize the most 
contextually relevant information for the final response. 
These enhancements improve both recall and precision in 
the retrieval stage, ensuring that the generative model operates 
on high-quality context without incurring significant latency.

IV. METHODOLOGY 
In this section, we detail the individual components of our 
proposed method, outlining the specific roles and processes of 
each. After describing document embedding, AI, front end, 
and back end, we synthesize these components in the final 
subsection, demon strating how they interact seamlessly 
through complete, practical use cases. 
A. Document Embedding 
In our system, all documents are ingested internally. Each 
document undergoes a standardized processing pipeline to 
ensure effective semantic search and retrieval. The main steps 
include: 
• Step 1 - Format Conversion:  
 Uploaded documents in .docx or .pdf format are first 
converted to markdown (.md) format for easier processing by 
large language models (LLMs). 
• Step 2 - Segmentation:  
 The markdown document is segmented by headers. If any 
segment exceeds a predefined maximum length, it is further 
split to ensure all segments are within the allowed token or 
character limit. 
• Step 3 - Semantic Embedding:  
 Each segment is then embedded using a semantic embed- 
ding model to enable efficient and meaningful semantic 
search. 
• Step 4 - Metadata Storage:  
 For each segment, metadata such as document title, page 
number, and position within the document is stored, 
supporting precise context tracking and flexible document 
retrieval. 
B. Artificial Intelligence (AI) 
In this section, we focus solely on the core AI logic and 
omit system scaling considerations. The core workflow 
consists of several sequential steps (also referred to as nodes 
in the pipeline): input preparation, information extraction, 
context retrieval, reranking, and answer generation & 
formatting. 
1) Input Preparation 
The process begins when a user query is received. The 
first AI node systematically constructs a distilled 
conversational memory for effective downstream processing. 
This involves: 
• Step 1 – Recent Message Aggregation:  
 If the conversation is at an early stage and contains only a 
few messages, the current query is combined with all previous 
messages to preserve complete context. 
• Step 2 – Memory Summarization:  
 When the conversation becomes lengthy, messages that 
are older than a certain threshold are summarized into a 
concise form known as memory, retaining important prior 
context while remaining within processing limits. 
• Step 3 – Input Combination:  
 The input for the next step is constructed by combining the 
memory summary (if available), the most recent conversation 
messages, and the current user query. 
 For example, if the user asks, “Expand the last section of 
your answer,” the model references the last answer, recent 
conversation history, and summarized memory to generate the 
requested expansion with relevant details. 
2) Information Extraction 
 In this step, the AI module extracts key information from 
the prepared memory and context: topic, task, and age. This 
process includes: 
• Step 1 – LLM Invocation:  
 The system calls the large language model (LLM) and 
requests a structured JSON response with fields topic, task, 
and age. 
• Step 2 – Response Processing:  
 The system interprets the extracted attributes to determine 
the workflow’s next action. 
 Handling of the extracted values proceeds as follows: 
- Case 1 - Missing Age:  
 If the topic is provided but the age is missing, the system 
prompts the user to specify the age. 
- Case 2 - Specified Topic and Age:  
 If both topic and age are specified, the system identifies 
the task. For certain predefined tasks (e.g., designing a lecture, 
expanding a section), a custom prompt is generated; 
otherwise, a general prompt is used. 
- Case 3 - Unrelated Topic:  
 If the topic is unrelated to any internal document, the 
language model enters a general mode and acts as a standard 
conversational agent.

 A key challenge here is ensuring that the LLM prioritizes 
the most recent conversational information. For example, if 
the user switches topics midway, the system should detect and 
update the current topic, age, and task accordingly. 
3) Context Retrieval  
 Once the key attributes have been determined, the system 
retrieves relevant contextual information to support an 
informed response. This node operates as follows: 
• Step 1 – Topic Filtering:  
 Using the extracted topic, the system filters the database to 
select only documents that belong to the identified topic, 
retrieving all corresponding segments. 
• Step 2 – Hybrid Retrieval:  
 Next, both keyword-based and semantic retrieval methods 
are applied to these filtered segments to identify the top k 
matches based on the current query and task. 
The goal of this process is to ensure that the response 
generated in later steps is grounded in the most relevant and 
accurate information available for the user’s needs. 
4) Reanking 
After retrieving potential knowledge segments, the system 
optimizes for relevance by reranking the candidates. This 
process includes: 
• Step 1 – Relevance Scoring:  
 Each retrieved document segment or answer candidate is 
evaluated using a re-ranker model. This re-ranker is an LLM 
specifically designed and fine- tuned for assessing the 
contextual relevance of candidates with respect to the current 
query and task. 
• Step 2 – Candidate Selection:   
 The top r candidates (r < k) with the highest relevance 
scores are selected for answer generation, ensuring that the 
response closely matches both the user’s intent and the given 
context. 
5) Answer Generation & Formatting 
In the final step, the synthesized context and user query are 
provided to the main language model (LLM) to generate a 
draft answer.  This output is then formatted to meet the 
requirements of both the identified task and the specified age.  
This process includes: 
• Step 1 – Answer Generation:  
 The main LLM is invoked with the constructed context 
and current query, producing a comprehensive answer aligned 
with the user’s needs. 
• Step 2 – Task-Based Formatting:  
 The response is formatted according to the outline, style, 
and requirements defined by the institution for the selected 
task. 
• Step 3 – Age Appropriateness:  
 The language, terminology, and depth are further 
formatted to suit the age specified by the user, enhancing both 
clarity and accessibility. 
C. Front End 
The front end serves as the primary interface between 
users and the system. In this project, we emphasize simplicity 
and focus on showcasing the AI component by implementing 
only the core features required for a smooth workflow and 
clear demonstration, while keeping auxiliary features to a 
minimum. The architecture is inspired by platforms such as 
OpenAI’s ChatGPT, featuring a straightforward 
registration/login page and a main chat session interface. 
1)  Authentication  
The authentication system secures access and personalizes 
user interactions. It comprises the following stages: 
• Sign-Up and Login: Users can create new accounts 
or log in using existing credentials through dedicated pages. 
All credential handling is performed securely via encrypted 
protocols. 
• Session Management: Upon successful 
authentication, the front end securely stores session tokens and 
manages session states, enabling users to remain logged in 
across visits and devices unless they log out or their session 
expires. 
2) Chat Interface  
 Once authenticated, users interact with the system’s core 
functionality through an intuitive chat interface designed for 
efficient communication with the AI: 
• Conversation Management: Users can start new 
conversations, revisit, or delete existing ones. Conversation 
histories are persistently stored and easily accessible via the 
interface. 
• Query Submission: Users submit natural language 
queries, which are transmitted seamlessly to the back end for 
processing. 
• AI Responses: AI-generated responses are displayed 
in a conversational format, enabling follow-up questions and 
contextual clarifications within the ongoing session. 
• Prompt Suggestions: The interface offers context -
aware prompt suggestions to assist users in formulating 
effective queries, improving efficiency and satisfaction. 
D. Back End 
The back end is responsible for managing all system 
functionalities beyond the core AI processing. Its primary 
roles include authenticating users, handling secure data 
storage and retrieval, and orchestrating workflows related to 
AI interactions. When a user submits an AI-related query, the 
back end  forwards the request to the AI component and 
coordinates all necessary data exchanges, acting as the central 
hub for operational logic. 
1) User Management  
User management ensures secure access control and 
session handling. This process includes: 
• Registration and Authentication: The back end 
securely processes user sign- up and login information, 
validating credentials and issuing authentication tokens in 
accordance with industry best practices. 
• Session Handling: User sessions are managed using 
these authentication tokens, with support for session 
expiration, renewal, and logout. This ensures both robust 
security and a seamless user experience. 
2) Data Management  
Reliable storage and retrieval of user and conversation 
data are crucial for personalized and context -aware AI 
interactions: 
• Profile Storage:  
 User profiles, along with relevant metadata for 
personalization and access control, are securely stored and 
managed. 
• Conversation History:  
 All conversations are persistently logged and efficiently 
indexed, enabling rapid retrieval and maintaining contextual 
continuity for the AI across multiple interactions and sessions.

V. EXPERIMENT 
A. Implementation Details 
This subsection provides technical specifics regarding the 
components and technologies used in our system, including 
language models, storage solutions, and the frameworks 
supporting the front and back end. Specifically, we use 
qwen2.5:14b for both embedding generation and as the core 
large language model, while mxbai -rerank-large-v2 is 
employed for reranking. Embeddings are stored and queried 
using ChromaDB, our vector database. For data 
preprocessing and orchestration, we leverage the MinerU 
package for d ocument processing, Langchain for chunking, 
and LangGraph as the orchestrator. The front end is built with 
React.js to provide an interactive user interface. On the back 
end, we use FastAPI to serve APIs, with JWT for 
authentication and SQLModel for database interactions.

VI. CONCLUSION 
In this work, we have presented a customized AI chatbot 
designed specifically for educational environments, 
advancing beyond the limitations of general -purpose large 
language models. Our system uniquely integrates rule-based 
filtering, retrieval-augmented g eneration (RAG), and user-  
adaptive response strategies, drawing upon curated, lesson -
aligned internal resources to deliver accurate, contextually 
relevant, and pedagogically consistent answers. By combining 
hybrid retrieval methods and contextual reranking, the chatbot 
is able to produce responses tailored to user attributes such as 
age group and specific educational tasks. Leveraging a high-
performing and compact open -source model (Qwen -14B) 
enables on -premises deployment on modest hardware, 
reducing operational costs and ensuring data privacy —key 
requirements for educational institutions. The robust 
workflow, which encompas ses advanced document 
processing, sophisticated AI orchestration, and seamless 
integration between the front end and back end, demonstrates 
the system’s ability to operate effectively in real -world 
settings.  
For future work, further opportunities lie in enhancing 
personalization through deeper user modeling, expanding the 
range of supported educational content, and scaling the system 
for use in larger or multi-lingual institutions. Overall, our 
method provides an effective, adaptable, and institutionally 
aligned conversational AI framework, contributing to the 
evolving landscape of educational technology.
